# Nuits Sonores 2025

- **Name:** Nuits Sonores (22nd edition, 28 May – 1 June 2025)
- **Business:** electronic and independent music festival run by the association Arty Farty; day and night programme across Lyon, main site Les Grandes Locos (former rail depot), plus the European Lab forum
- **Place:** Lyon, France
- **Year:** 2025 (published by DIA Sept 2025; press Nov–Dec 2025)
- **Studio:** DIA (Dreamers Ink Aesthetics; Mitch Paone creative director, New York / Chamonix), generative tool with creative technologist André Burnier, type from DIA's own foundry MNKY Type. Festival side: Julien Roche (visual artistic director), Mathis Alexandre.

## Brief & business truth
Nuits Sonores has run since 2003 and invites a different designer each year to dress the edition. The brief was open except for one fixed asset: the existing NS circular logo. The festival also asked for software it could run itself, so the in-house team could produce the hundreds of assets a city-wide festival needs (posters, lineups, artist cards, signage, screens) after the studio left. The problem: make one edition feel physically loud across a whole city, and keep that feeling consistent when non-designers generate the assets.

## Strategy / insight
Music at a festival is felt in the body before it is read; so the identity should behave like sound, not depict it. DIA's position is "kinetic identity": motion is the foundation and stills are frames captured from a moving system. They explicitly steered away from rave clichés (saturated rainbows, trance lettering, glitch) toward Swiss-style order plus generative behaviour.

## The idea (one sentence)
Letters are made of particles that pulse, smear and scatter as if pushed by a speaker cone, held in check by one rigid grotesque that keeps time like a metronome.
**Source:** moment (the physical experience of the sound in the room), rendered through a custom tool.

## Register
Loud display / generative kinetic (black-and-white techno-punk with a single acid accent).

## Category codes used or broken
- Used: black/white night palette, huge display letters, lineup poster with dense artist list, merch.
- Broken: no photography of crowds or DJs on the key visual, no psychedelic gradients or multi-colour neon, no glitch/VHS texture; one flat fluorescent green instead of a spectrum. The mark of the year is a behaviour, not a picture.

## Mark
- **Fixed logo (inherited):** NS monogram in a circle, a thin monoline ring enclosing a stylised N whose diagonal doubles as an S, set small; used in green as a sign-off on every item (poster corner, artist cards, signage centre).
- **Edition "mark":** the words NUITS SONORES set in MNKY Klaus capitals, each letter rotated 90° and stacked vertically on posters (reading bottom-to-top), then fed through the particle simulation so strokes dissolve into combed, symmetrical smears (they read like sound-wave oscilloscope traces or Rorschach blots). Letter cores stay solid enough to read; the dissolution happens at the ends and edges.
- Variants: horizontal wordmark (two rows NUITS / SONORES with the NS roundel in the gap), vertical poster stack, single words (e.g. BAR) for wayfinding.

## Wordmark / lettering
MNKY Klaus (MNKY Type, by Mitch Paone): rigid monoline grotesque, architectural, minimal stroke modulation, straight-legged R. Tested in early simulations for distortion tolerance so it stays legible when particles tear it. Caps for display; mixed case for information.

## Type system
- One family does everything. Display: Klaus caps, particle-treated, black on white. Information: Klaus in mixed case, solid neon green, set tight (lineup posters show lines at roughly 1.0 leading with superscript tags "live", "b2b", "dj set" in a smaller size as typographic modifiers).
- Rule (inferred from applications): information type is never particle-treated; only display words dissolve. Green information type is laid directly over the black particle image (overprint), so the two layers read as foreground rhythm and background noise.
- Type-as-image: the festival name is the key visual; there is no separate illustration.

## Colour system
- Black and white as the base (white ground for print, black ground for screens and night).
- One accent: neon/fluorescent green (hex not published; visually close to pure RGB green, approx #2BFF00 on screen, a fluorescent ink in print (inferred)).
- Roles: black = particle image; green = all information (dates, lineup, URLs, logo); white = ground. The green never becomes a background fill in the core posters (inferred).
- The green escaped the page: audiences wore it (clothes, glowsticks, make-up, hair dye) and production turned stage fabrics and lasers green.

## Device(s) / graphic language
- **Particle generator** (built on Burnier's fluid-dynamics simulations in Processing): parameters include emission rate, particle decay, drag/friction, cluster formation and dispersal, a central rhythmic pulse; mouse interaction pushes particles like a crowd moving. Presets tuned per format (poster, banner, signage, mobile) with explicit density thresholds so every output stays in family. Outputs very high-resolution stills and motion in any aspect ratio.
- **Symmetry:** smears are mirrored around each letter's axis, which gives an organic but ordered, almost biological pattern.
- **Rotation:** vertical stacking of the name on portrait formats; horizontal rows on landscape.
- **Artist cards:** black frame, artist photo treated with the same horizontal particle smear on edges, name in green caps top-centre, venue and time rotated along the side edges, NS roundel bottom-centre.

## Imagery
- Artist portraits only in social cards, framed in black with the particle smear bleeding into the photo edges. Event photography (after the fact) shows the green in lighting and crowd.
- Never: crowd shots as key visual, stock club imagery, colour gradients.

## Voice
Mostly informational and bilingual-light French (dates "28 mai – 1er juin", "Lyon, France"); the words that matter are the lineup. Tags like "live", "b2b" are treated as typographic parts. Festival copy about the identity speaks in terms of circulation, cadence and vibration.

## Applications
Posters (lineup + key visual pair), metro and city lightboxes, monumental scaffold banners at Les Grandes Locos, wayfinding (particle words such as BAR), stage screens and VJ loops, social artist cards and teasers, printed programme (editorial spreads), t-shirts and merch, client tool UI.
**What varies:** particle state (every frame is different), orientation of the name, format-specific density presets. **What is fixed:** Klaus, the black/white/green triad, the NS roundel, green for information.

## Guidelines structure
No public guideline document. The "guidelines" are embedded in the client tool: calibrated presets, density limits, and the rule that Klaus anchors every composition.

## What makes it ownable
The combed, mirrored particle smear of the letters plus one fluorescent green. Either alone identifies the edition; together they are unmistakable, which is why the audience could "wear" the brand with just the colour.

## What a generic version would have been
A photo of hands in the air under lasers, a gradient purple-pink-cyan palette, chrome or glitch display type, a lineup in a condensed sans. Every European techno festival poster of the period.

## Principles it demonstrates
- Make the mark behave the way the product feels (sound pushes air, so particles push letters) instead of illustrating it.
- Pair one wild generative layer with one rigid typeface; the fixed element is what lets the variable one run free.
- Limit colour to base plus one accent strong enough that people can carry it into the world themselves.
- Ship the system as a tool with tuned presets and density limits, so the client's output stays on-brand after handover.
- Keep information and expression on separate layers with separate rules (information never distorted).
