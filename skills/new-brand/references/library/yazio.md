# Yazio

- **Name / business / place / year / studio:** YAZIO, calorie and nutrition-tracking app (freemium, with Pro subscription), headquartered in Linz, Austria; described as Europe's leading nutrition app. Launched March–April 2026. Identity by Koto (London). Custom typeface Yazio Sans by Hottype (Zagreb, Croatia). Koto also trained a custom LLM for in-app copy in the two brand voices.

## Brief & business truth
A large, successful but visually tired and inconsistent app in a crowded category (MyFitnessPal, Lose It!, Noom and dozens of "functional lookalikes": green leaves, charts, salad photos). Yazio was introducing a gamified product (streaks, wins, badges) to drive habit formation and retention, and needed an identity that could live inside that product, not just around it. The truth Koto built on: healthy habits break when they feel restrictive; people quit diets that feel like punishment.

## Strategy / insight
"Good Dopamine": make healthy eating feel rewarding in the moment, like a small win, so the habit sticks. Reposition Yazio from a calorie ledger to an energising companion that swaps pressure for play.

## The idea (one sentence)
Your health hang-ups are a big hairy monster that turns out to be adorable: Yettie, a horned, clawed yeti who is all bark and no bite, makes the scary part of changing habits cuddly and cheers every small win.
**Source:** benefit (Good Dopamine: the reward of small wins) expressed through a reversal (the scary monster is sweet); the name's "y" and "yeti" sound helped (inferred).

## Register
Character world: a mascot-led, gamified, bright-on-dark digital brand with chunky display type.

## Category codes it used or broke
- Broke: diet/fitness codes of discipline, before/after bodies, green-leaf clean-eating, clinical charts, guilt. The brand is cuddly, joking and forgiving ("off days" are fine).
- Broke: the category's pale, white, airy UI look; Yazio uses a soft near-black ground with electric turquoise.
- Used: gamification codes (streaks, badges, trophies, fire), now carried by the character (Yettie on fire for a streak, frozen in an ice cube for a paused one).
- Used knowingly: the mascot playbook of Duolingo, but with a creature whose look contradicts its temperament, which gives it a joke to tell.

## Mark
- **Type:** character + lowercase wordmark lockup (character left, wordmark right); character alone as app icon and avatar (inferred for icon).
- **Yettie's construction:** a pear-shaped, bottom-heavy body with two short column legs with rounded feet, arms hanging to the sides, small yellow-green claws (three points) at the hands and feet. A big white rounded-rectangle face mask set high, framed by a fringe of scalloped fur at the top; two small oval black eyes, a wide open mouth with a row of small rounded teeth and a pink tongue. Two short curved horns in lime-yellow gradient and two pointed ears sticking out sideways. Fur is suggested by short strokes in a lighter tone over a turquoise-to-green vertical gradient, lighter at the belly and head (a soft glow). Silhouette readable from horns + ears + wide base alone.
- **Variants:** full-body standing pose (lockup), a library of action poses (waving a flag, chef with recipe book, lifting, celebrating in confetti, carrying food, meditating on a cloud, sleeping, with a water streak meter), fire/ice states for streaks, a menacing red-eyed silhouette for "Junk food beware", tiny Yettie heads that pop through type. Poses also flatten into functional icons and hiking-patch-style badges.

## Wordmark / lettering
Custom lowercase "yazio" in a very heavy, rounded, closed-form sans (from Yazio Sans, drawn with Hottype). Corners are softened, counters small (the a's counter is a narrow horizontal slot, the o's a small circle in a thick ring). "Smile" curves are built in: the y's tail sweeps left in a grin-like arc and the z's diagonal is a bowed, swooping stroke. Tight letter spacing; off-white (#FFFEF9 sampled) on soft black.

## Type system
- **Yazio Sans** (custom, Hottype): display and headlines. Heavy weight, rounded, closed apertures, big curves, grotesque details with quirks (y with a single-stroke flat-bottomed bowl, curved z). Set big, tight leading, sentence case, often two or three lines filling the frame.
- **Noto Sans** (Google): functional UI and supporting copy.
- Type-as-image: headlines are a stage for Yettie, who sleeps on letters, pops out of counters and sits between words; oversized pale words in the background as texture behind the app ("Nutritional values", "Meals", "Snacks").

## Colour system
- Mostly black and white: a soft black (≈ #181617 sampled) and warm off-white (≈ #FAF8F2 / #FFFEF9).
- Accent colours drawn from Yettie's fur: electric turquoise-mint (≈ #00FFC3 sampled as a full field), fur green (≈ #09DAA9), lime-yellow highlights on horns and claws (≈ #CEFA85). Official hex values not published.
- Gradients: vertical turquoise-to-green with a light glow, used on the character and occasionally as fields.
- State colours inside the product: orange-red fire for streaks, icy blue-violet for frozen streaks, pink for the tongue.
- Proportion (inferred): neutral grounds dominate; turquoise mostly lives in Yettie, with an occasional full turquoise field (type specimen, hero).

## Device(s) / graphic language
- **Yettie as system:** the main device, deployed as poses, icons, badges and state indicators; each pose has one prop that says what the moment is.
- **Hiking-patch badges:** achievements drawn as outdoor-club patches (the yeti lives in mountains).
- **Floating food:** 3D-rendered everyday foods (bread, chocolate, broccoli, carrot, acorn, cheese, apple) scattered around the edges as a frame, both healthy and indulgent.
- **Interruption:** Yettie breaks into typography.
- **Motion principles:** alive and responsive; celebrate big and small wins; occasionally burst into brilliance.

## Imagery
Illustration and soft 3D rendering, no photography of bodies, plates or athletes in the published set. Foods are rendered glossy and toy-like, including indulgent ones, which signals no food shaming.

## Voice
Two voices with rules for who speaks when: Yazio (clear, helpful, informative: numbers, instructions) and Yettie (cheeky, warm, encouraging: celebrations, nudges, setbacks). Non-judgemental, friendly, cuddly. Sample lines: "Healthy habits don't bite", "Off days, nope days, just-not-happening days", "Junk food beware", "55 days streak!", "2 days frozen". Koto ran a copy workshop with Yazio's writers and trained an LLM to produce copy in each voice.

## Applications
App (Today summary with calorie ring, macros, streaks), streak cards, achievement badges and icons, social and campaign key visuals, out-of-home posters, launch merch (plush-friendly character), motion.
What varies: Yettie's pose, prop and mood (even menace), ground (black/off-white/turquoise), foods around the frame. What stays fixed: Yettie's face and silhouette, Yazio Sans heavy display, turquoise as Yettie's colour, the rule of which voice speaks.

## Guidelines structure
Not public. Case imagery shows: character sheet and pose library, wordmark lockup, typeface specimen, colour, iconography/badges, motion principles, tone of voice for two speakers (inferred from the case and press).

## What makes it ownable
Yettie's silhouette (horns + side ears + white face mask + turquoise gradient fur); the monster-that-isn't joke; the rounded ultra-heavy lowercase wordmark; turquoise-on-soft-black in a category of white and leaf green.

## What a generic version would have been
A green leaf or apple symbol, a clean geometric sans wordmark, white UI with pastel charts, food photography of salads and smoothies, motivational fitness copy.

## Principles it demonstrates
- Give a mascot a built-in contradiction (scary look, sweet temperament) so it has a joke and a point of view, not just a face.
- Draw the mascot as a system of poses, props and states that do product work (streaks, badges, empty states), not decoration.
- Take the brand colour from the character so the character is the colour.
- Write rules for who speaks when: a clear brand voice for facts, a character voice for emotion.
- Let the display typeface share the character's shapes (round, closed, heavy) so type and mascot feel drawn by one hand.
- Show indulgent and healthy things together to signal a non-judgemental stance without saying it.
