## Brief & business truth
Two founders with over 25 years in optometry wanted optical retail that feels like a destination: eye care, retail and hospitality in one, modelled on international stores that treat customers as guests. They needed clinical credibility and a design-led fashion experience at the same time. Diagnostic equipment is hidden (behind a dichroic glass wall); a welcome table comes before the product wall.

## Strategy / insight
The client already called the space a "brand temple". AFOM built on that: a temple where sight is revered, and a theatre where you try on characters. Eyewear changes how the world sees you; the brand frames that as performance.

## The idea (one sentence) and its source
The store is a temple to sight: a carved-stone logotype and a temple marque whose column capitals are also eyes and lenses, then made theatrical by overprinting that temple across models' eyes. **Source:** place (the store as a temple), with the product's benefit (sight) built into its geometry.

## Register
Material luxury with classical, carved restraint, made playful by fashion theatre.

## Category codes it used or broke
- Broke: clinical white-and-blue optometry, eye-chart graphics, smiling models in glasses, discount signage, crowded frame walls.
- Used and re-cut: the eye (every optical brand's symbol) becomes a concentric Ionic volute; the eye test becomes theatre; the frame wall is kept but treated as a lit gallery in gold.

## Mark
- **Temple marque:** monoline outline drawing with one uniform stroke weight (about 1/40 of the marque's width, inferred) and square joins. A shallow pediment (roof pitch about 13 degrees, inferred) sits on a rectangular body. Across the middle runs a row of four equal circles, each a set of concentric rings: outer ring, inner ring, solid centre dot, referencing Ionic capitals and reading as eyes or lenses. The two outer circles are joined to the side walls by quarter-circle notches that bite into the body (like column capitals meeting an entablature); the two inner circles are linked by a small rectangular frame, which also reads as the bridge of a pair of glasses. Result: temple, column tops, two pairs of eyes, and spectacles in one figure.
- **Logotype:** SIXSIX set as one word in capitals, in a flared glyphic serif: strokes swell toward wedge-like terminals rather than bracketed serifs, like incised stone lettering; moderate contrast; wide S's, a narrow I, an X whose strokes cross with flared ends. Subtle details "as though carved from stone" (the studio's wording, paraphrased).
- Variants: logotype alone (most uses); marque alone (window, flag, poster lockups); marque small above address stack; marque at large scale overprinted in white across faces.

## Wordmark / lettering
Capitals only, no space between the words, normal tracking. Embossed or debossed on paper stock to make the carved reference literal.

## Type system
- Flared glyphic serif: the logotype only (inferred: no text setting in it).
- Bold grotesque sans: the information stack, always centred, mixed case with the key word in capitals: "Eyewear / OPTOMETRY / Onsite Lab / 112 LT. COLLINS". The same stack appears on banners, posters, windows and social.
- Occasional small colour callouts in social ("NEW IN Balmain" in yellow over a portrait).

## Colour system
- Warm yellow, #FAE645 (sampled from AFOM's published marque artwork): the core brand colour, developed with Kennedy Nolan to flatter skin tones and cast shoppers in a warm glow.
- Black: marque, logotype and type on yellow.
- White: logotype and marque reversed over photography.
- Interior: tawny gold used monochromatically (walls, drapes, carpet, joinery), with yellow travertine, jarrah and brass.
- Pairings: black on yellow; white on photo; the identity never adds a second accent (inferred).

## Device(s) / graphic language
1. **Marque over the eyes:** the temple marque printed in white across a model's eyes, obscuring them; the brand becomes the lens through which identity is seen.
2. **Stepped yellow field:** layouts where a yellow panel steps like a staircase or plinth (an L-shaped block), holding the logotype twice or the information stack, with a portrait set into the step.
3. **Poster grid:** a repeated portrait format: logotype across the top, face in the centre, marque and info stack small at bottom left; nine or more variants tiled.
4. **Idents:** short animations between films weave marque, type and logotype into mini "ad breaks".
5. **The fur screen:** an oversized screen upholstered in fluffy white fur in the window, playing the films and idents; a white-fur plinth carries a dimensional logotype.

## Imagery
Fashion portraits in grey studio light, deadpan and theatrical: models of wide age range (an older woman with long grey hair is a lead), each playing a small character moment (sucking a lollipop, grimacing, crying, pulling faces). Frames are the only colour accent. Films are vignettes with custom soundtracks and subtitles designed to stop a scroll. Interiors photographed in total gold monochrome.

## Voice
Deadpan, theatrical, witty in films and subtitles (one subtitle reads "Back. Forth. Back. Forth."); informational signage is terse and factual (the four-line stack). Rules (inferred): humour lives in the moving image and captions; the static identity stays composed.

## Applications
Storefront window with fur screen and illuminated logotype plinth, projecting flag banner, interior (gold drapes, frame wall, welcome table), carrier bags (white with centred logotype), embossed stationery, posters, social grid, films and idents, lens cloth/case (gold tube with engraved logotype), website.
- **Fixed:** logotype, marque, yellow, the centred info stack, the portrait style.
- **Varies:** character in each portrait/film, crop and scale of the marque (small signature vs. mask over the eyes), material (emboss, fur, brass, print).

## What makes it ownable
The temple-with-eyes marque; SIXSIX in carved capitals; the warm yellow; the marque masking models' eyes; the fur screen.

## What a generic version would have been
A thin sans wordmark with a pair-of-glasses icon or an eye symbol, white and teal clinical palette, eye-chart letters, glossy models smiling in frames, and a brand-logo wall.

## Principles it demonstrates
- Take the client's own metaphor for the place (a temple) literally and build the symbol from its architecture.
- Hide the benefit (sight) inside the geometry so the symbol works on two levels.
- Choose the brand colour with the architect and the customer's skin in mind; colour is a physical experience in retail.
- Keep the static identity composed and let motion, casting and copy carry the humour.
- Use the symbol as an action (masking eyes) rather than only as a sign-off.
