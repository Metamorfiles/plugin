# Mozilla

- **Name / business / place / year / studio:** Mozilla (the non-profit Mozilla Foundation and its for-profit Mozilla Corporation: Firefox, Thunderbird, Mozilla.ai, Mozilla Ventures, advocacy). San Francisco / global. Launched September 2024 (rolled out through 2024–25). Identity by Jones Knowles Ritchie (JKR, London/New York; global ECD Lisa Smith), on a brand strategy written with DesignStudio in 2023. Custom type by Studio DRAMA (Mozilla Headline / Semi-Slab, Mozilla Sans / Text). Awards on the type: D&AD Wood Pencil, ADC, Creative Review Annual.

## Brief & business truth
Mozilla is a 1998-born, open-source, mission-driven organisation whose public recognition had collapsed into one product: Firefox. Staff and public alike said "Firefox" when they meant Mozilla; reported brand equity for Mozilla itself sat around 25%. Meanwhile Mozilla was expanding into trustworthy AI, investment, advocacy and new products, and needed one master brand that could hold them all without diminishing Firefox. The previous identity (the 2017 "moz://a" URL-protocol wordmark) was clever but read as a developer in-joke and did not travel to activism or government audiences. Audiences range from grassroots coders and activists to policymakers, which the system had to serve at once.

## Strategy / insight
The internet was built as a place of sharing, discovery and delight and has become one run for corporate interests, where people feel watched and powerless. Mozilla's role is to lead the fight to give it back: the brand promise "Reclaim the Internet" (control of time, privacy, expression, community and wonder). The system was framed as "Grassroots to Government": fixed, sober assets for institutional audiences, looser expressive use for activist ones, built on the same parts.

## The idea (one sentence)
Mozilla plants a flag: the M becomes a protest flag that, with one displaced pixel, is also the head of Mozilla's old T. rex, so the symbol of a movement doubles as the memory of the early, open web.
**Source:** people/cause (the activist movement) crossed with the company's own heritage (the Shepard Fairey dinosaur and early-internet pixel culture); secondarily the name (the M).

## Register
Activist editorial: black-and-white protest-poster seriousness with an early-internet pixel/terminal accent. Loud display in expression, strict in structure.

## Category codes it used or broke
- Broke: tech's sea of geometric sans and friendly rounded blue. Mozilla leads with a semi-slab serif, black and white, and one acid RGB green.
- Broke: the "product logo" habit. The master brand is a flag (a thing you carry and plant), not an app icon or a fox.
- Used, on purpose: pixel grids, ASCII art, blinking text cursors (early-internet nostalgia) as a signal of the open web Mozilla wants back.
- Used: the grid-heavy report look of policy/think-tank publishing, so it can address governments credibly.

## Mark
- **Type:** symbol (flag) + separate wordmark; the symbol is dynamic (flag ↔ T. rex).
- **Construction (from JKR's construction frame):** a vertical pole as a plain rectangle stroke, roughly twice the flag's height, standing at the left. Attached to its top right is the flag, which is the letter M turned 90° onto its side: its two stems become the flag's top and bottom edges (heavy horizontal bars) and its V becomes a chevron notch cut into the fly end, pointing back toward the pole. Just inside the top-left corner of the flag sits one detached square pixel, separated from the bars by a gap of about one pixel. Everything is orthogonal except the notch's diagonals; widths are equal-stroke and square-ended.
- **Transformation:** in motion the pole bends into a curved neck/body and a baseline appears; the pixel reads as an eye and the notch as an open jaw, so the flag becomes the T. rex head (the "roar"). An ASCII-art rendering of the rex is the literal mascot version.
- **Optical details:** the pixel is the key accent; it references the eye of Shepard Fairey's 1998 dinosaur and "the pixel" as the atom of the digital world. The notch keeps the flag from reading as a plain E/Σ.
- **Variants:** flag alone (app icon, badges, lanyards, merch), flag + wordmark lockup (wordmark left, symbol to its right, cap-height aligned in decks), animated flag-to-rex; symbol at huge scale cropped off the bottom edge on badges (inferred as a supported crop from the application images).

## Wordmark / lettering
Custom: "Mozilla" in upper/lower case in a heavy, tightly spaced semi-slab from the Mozilla type family. Flat-sided rounded-square o, bracketless stubby slab feet on l and i, single-storey a with a spur, M with vertical outer stems. Solid black on white (or white/green reversed). It sits as a compact block between two baseline/cap guides; no symbol inside the letters.

## Type system
- **Mozilla Headline (Semi-Slab):** protest-poster headline face; slabs reduced to short stubs so it reads modern. Weights ExtraLight–Bold. Used large, often very large, set tight, sometimes cropped off the canvas edge (keynote screens show giant letters partly covered by portrait tiles).
- **Mozilla Text / Sans (Sans Text):** accessibility-first companion for body, UI and data; glyphs drawn to reduce confusion (distinct l, i, 1). Weights ExtraLight–Bold.
- **Uniwidth (duplexed):** every weight occupies the same width, so text can change weight on hover or in animation without reflowing, which is also what lets the type pulse and "type itself" with a blinking cursor in motion.
- Sans and serif are declared interchangeable for greater range; the slab is the voice, the sans the explainer.
- Type-as-image: huge single words ("RISE") at stage scale; numerals as report heroes ("04", "70"); lines typed out with a cursor.

## Colour system
- Foundation: black (sampled ≈ #161616) and white (≈ #EFEFEF off-white on print) do most of the work.
- Accent: Mozilla Green, a classic RGB-screen green (sampled ≈ #20CB47 from JKR's badge image; official hex not published outside the portal).
- A wider green range plus darker tones and pastels (pink, orange, coral seen behind portraits) for illustration, infographics and expressive "grassroots" pieces.
- Proportions (inferred from applications): black or white ground ~70–80%, green as a single accent block or single-colour print, secondary colours only as small portrait-backing tiles.
- Stated principles for colour: insightful, optimistic, fearless.

## Device(s) / graphic language
- **Pixel grid:** a square modular grid underlies layouts; content sits in grid cells, and portraits are cut into square or rectangular tiles placed on it.
- **Visible structure:** thin hairline rules and cell outlines are left visible in reports and decks, like graph paper or a terminal.
- **Icons:** built from the flag's vocabulary (square strokes, pixel accent).
- **ASCII / pixel art:** the mascot and some illustrations rendered as characters or pixels; line-art wireframes (a wireframe eye for "open source AI").
- **Cursor:** blinking text cursor as a motion device; headlines type on.
- Rules: Government-grade material uses the fixed assets only (symbol, wordmark, grid, black/white/green); grassroots material may stretch expression (cropping, scale, colour tiles, mascot) on top of the same parts.

## Imagery
Warm, candid portraits of real, diverse people, cut out or tightly cropped and set in solid colour tiles (green, pink, orange, coral); head-and-shoulders, smiling or mid-speech. Documentary photos of people speaking or working, placed in grid cells. No stock tech clichés (no glowing circuits, no padlocks) visible in the published set (inferred).

## Voice
"Brainy bestie": expertise made accessible and culturally relevant, using humour to get people to act. Imperative, collective lines: "Reclaim the internet with us". Short slogan-like headlines in the slab, longer explanatory copy in the sans.

## Applications
Staff badges and lanyards (white, green, black variants of the same giant cropped flag), water bottle with green cap and slogan plus a sticker flag, caps and hoodies (green wordmark on black), keynote stage screens, reports (AI, sustainability, diversity), decks and web templates, social tiles, stickers and merch.
What varies: ground colour (black/white/green), scale and crop of the flag, portrait tiles, how expressive the type gets. What stays fixed: flag and wordmark drawing, the two typefaces, black/white/green core, the grid.

## Guidelines structure
Published as a brand portal (brand.mozilla.com, Frontify) that holds all Mozilla brands; JKR's guideline pages shown in the launch post cover: logo and flag, wordmark, typography, colour, grid and layout, photography, symbols/icons, and templates for reports and decks (from image evidence; the portal text could not be read).

## What makes it ownable
The sideways-M flag with its single pixel; the flag-to-T. rex transformation; the semi-slab in a tech category of sans; black + one RGB green.

## What a generic version would have been
A rounded geometric-sans wordmark with a gradient globe or shield symbol, a blue-purple "trust" palette, abstract network-line illustrations, and "privacy-first" stock photography of people on laptops.

## Principles it demonstrates
- Build the symbol from the initial plus one meaningful extra (here a pixel) so it carries both the name and the story.
- Make the mark an object with a job (a flag is carried, planted and waved) so every application has a natural action.
- Let a heritage character survive as a hidden reading of the new mark rather than as a separate mascot.
- Choose a typeface genre the category avoids (slab in tech) and give it one functional feature (uniwidth) that also powers motion.
- Split a system into fixed assets for institutions and flexible expression for grassroots use, both built from the same parts.
- Hold colour to black, white and one saturated accent, so the accent owns recognition.
