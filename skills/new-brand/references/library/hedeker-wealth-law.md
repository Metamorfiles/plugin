# Hedeker Wealth & Law

- **Name:** Hedeker Wealth & Law (sub-brands Hedeker Wealth, Hedeker Law)
- **Business:** small independent US group (Lincolnshire, Illinois) giving families investment management, financial planning, tax and legal/estate advice under one roof
- **Place:** Lincolnshire, IL, USA (studio in London, UK)
- **Year:** 2016 (BP&O 1 June 2016; Mindsparkle 6 June 2016)

## Brief & business truth
A regional wealth firm competing with specialists that each offer one service. Its difference is breadth: four disciplines (investment, management, financial planning, tax/legal) coordinated for one family across life stages. It wanted to move upmarket and attract a more affluent clientele, including younger wealth, without looking like a stuffy bank.

## Strategy / insight
Affluent clients judge an adviser by the same signals they judge any luxury service: material quality, restraint and craft. So keep the finance category's trusted codes (navy, serif, crest-like seal) but render them with contemporary geometry and tactile print, and make the "four disciplines, one family office" story visible in the mark. Socio said they deliberately embraced finance blue instead of avoiding it.

## The idea (one sentence)
Four interlaced loops (the four areas of expertise) weave into a single seal that hides an H at its centre: the name and the offer in one knot.
**Source:** name (the H) combined with the benefit (integration of four services).

## Register
Material luxury / heritage-modern (serif caps, navy and chocolate board, copper foil, uncoated coloured papers).

## Category codes used or broken
- Used: navy blue, classic serif, crest/seal-like emblem, stationery as the main touchpoint.
- Twisted: the seal is a monoline geometric lattice rather than a heraldic shield; navy is paired with "bitter chocolate" and copper rather than gold; the client newsletter is a newspaper with loud condensed headlines.
- BP&O's critique: the marque is not especially distinctive on its own, but its geometry, balanced counters and even stroke modernise the heraldic register.

## Mark
- **Type:** symbol (marque) + logotype.
- **Construction:** a monoline figure on a square module. Core: a 3 × 3 lattice of equal square cells (four vertical and four horizontal lines). On each of the four sides, two stadium-shaped loops (each two cells wide with semicircular ends of radius one cell) overlap by one cell, so each side forms two round lobes with a small pointed vesica where they cross. The result reads as a rounded quatrefoil/rosette, roughly five cells across. Stroke weight uniform, about one-eighth of a cell (measured). The central column plus middle bar can be read as an H (hidden H claimed by the studio; exact reading inferred).
- **Clear space:** X = height of the marque, with ½X margins shown on guideline page 1.1.
- **Colour:** copper/bronze on white; copper foil on navy or chocolate; white foil on dark.
- **Variants:** marque alone (seal on covers, folders, envelope flaps), logotype alone, marque-over-logotype lockup, sub-brand lockup (HED... WEALTH / LAW with the marque cropped at the edge, guideline page 1.3).

## Wordmark / lettering
HEDEKER in Plantin (Monotype) roman capitals, wide positive tracking, set small and quiet in grey or white. Sub-brand descriptor (WEALTH, LAW) in smaller tracked caps under a very large, cropped HED on guideline examples. No modified letters.

## Type system
- **Plantin** (Light, Medium, Bold): primary face for logotype, body text, numerals, big display numbers (e.g. 6.1 / 8.4 / 5.7 in the newspaper).
- **Johnston** (secondary sans): small functional text (per BP&O).
- **Rhode Condensed** (Font Bureau): only in the Capital newspaper for loud, tightly-leaded all-caps headlines ("WILL THE FEDS RAISE THE RATES", "DOWN"), sometimes struck through with a copper rule.
- Rules visible in guidelines: stationery on a column grid with the type block filling the right-hand column; editorial on a two-column grid; infographics as fine line and dot charts in copper and grey.
- Type-as-image: the CAPITAL masthead is split into single spaced letters C A / P I T / A L arranged over the cover photograph.

## Colour system (published in guidelines, section 2.2)
- Royal Blue #2C374B (RGB 45/55/75, Pantone 541U), primary
- Bitter Chocolate #443833 (RGB 70/55/50, Pantone 476U), primary
- Copper RGB 190/132/92 (≈ #BE845C, Pantone 876U metallic), accent, used as foil and marque colour
- Cool Grey (RGB 200/200/200, Pantone Cool Grey 6U), support
- White
- Rule: the two primaries were chosen to match Colorplan paper stocks, so colour is carried by the paper itself, not by ink floods. Copper is the only "light" accent; never large areas.

## Device(s) / graphic language
- **Copper foil:** the marque and CAPITAL masthead are block-foiled copper; logotype also appears white-foiled on dark stock.
- **Dot pattern:** a fine field of copper dots in a repeating geometric arrangement (derived from the lattice nodes, inferred) on navy covers, letter backs and guideline cover; softens the formality.
- **Colour-dyed stocks:** navy and chocolate uncoated boards for cards and folders; a copper metallic board for the document folder with a white label.
- **Belly bands:** chocolate and navy wrap-bands on documents ("Documents Enclosed").

## Imagery
Capital newspaper: black-and-white photography of American institutional architecture (columns, flags, facades) and dramatic landscape (a mountain in cloud) as metaphors for markets and stability; low contrast, printed in greyscale under copper foil. Guidelines: image zone must be black and white, low contrast. Never: colour stock photos of smiling couples or handshakes.

## Voice
Reassuring, professional, legible (BP&O's summary). Capital speaks in plain market questions ("Will the Feds raise the rates or not?") and big figures. Headline tone is newsy and direct; body is measured.

## Applications
Letterhead, compliment slips, envelopes, business cards (navy and chocolate Colorplan, white foil), copper document folder with label, belly bands, guideline book, Capital quarterly newspaper (covers and spreads with charts), website.
**What varies:** paper colour per item and per newspaper edition (each Capital edition printed on a different stock), photography, headline scale in Capital. **What stays fixed:** Plantin caps logotype, copper marque, the navy/chocolate/copper triad, grids.

## What makes it ownable
The copper rosette seal on dark coloured board, and the split-letter copper CAPITAL masthead over grey photography. The palette (navy + chocolate + copper rather than navy + gold) is a distinct variant of the category colour.

## What a generic version would have been
A navy shield or column icon with a gold serif wordmark, glossy white paper, stock photos of families on a beach, "Your future, secured".

## Principles it demonstrates
- Keep the category's trust codes and win on execution: a better blue, a better serif, better paper.
- Choose brand colours from the paper stocks you will print on, so colour is physical.
- Let one metallic accent do all the luxury work, used small and always as foil.
- Encode the offer's structure (four services, one family office) in the mark's geometry, and let the name letter hide inside it.
- Give the brand a louder editorial voice in a separate channel (the newspaper) so the core identity can stay quiet.
