# The voice chart

The examples below come from Lumen Skincare, Studio's fictional example brand.

`## Voice` in DESIGN.md is a chart, not a paragraph. A paragraph describes a voice; a chart is
something a template can write to, and something the reviewer can hold a headline against.

## Shape

Three concepts. Not two, not six — three is enough to steer a writer and few enough to remember.
Each one is a real principle of this brand, named in a single word or two.

```md
## Voice

### Calm
Unhurried, warm, never urgent.

Do: "Two minutes in the morning. Then get on with your day."
Don't: "Hurry, your skin can't wait!"

### Specific
One benefit, with a number or a time.

Do: "Brighter skin in two weeks."
Don't: "Unlock your most radiant glow ever."

### Honest
Says what the product does, and stops there.

Do: "Invisible protection with no white cast."
Don't: "A clinically proven miracle for flawless skin."
```

The line under each concept is its characteristics: three adjectives, or one short sentence.
`Do:` and `Don't:` may repeat; a concept with two of each is fine.

## Where the lines come from

**Every `Do` is quoted from the brand's own copy** — its site, its app, its README, its docs. It is
evidence, like a hex value, and it is recorded in Sources like one. If a concept has no real line
behind it, it is not one of this brand's three concepts yet; say so in Known gaps rather than
writing the line yourself.

**Every `Don't` is yours to write.** It is the plausible wrong version — what a template would
produce if nobody had written the chart. Make it close enough to be tempting. A `Don't` nobody
would ever write teaches nothing.

## Tone

Under the chart, three rows and no more. Voice does not change; tone is how it lands in a given
place.

```md
| Context | Tone |
|---|---|
| Launch | Plain. Names the product and its one benefit. |
| Ad | Shorter still. One claim, one price. |
| Night range | Quieter. Fewer words, lower light. |
```

## What it is for

Every template that writes copy checks its headline and its call to action against the chart before
rendering. The reviewer does the same. A kit whose Voice section is prose gives them nothing to
check, which is how templates end up writing "Elevate your workflow" over a brand that never once
said "elevate".

## When a brand has no copy to quote

A logo and a palette and nothing written: leave `## Voice` out rather than inventing three concepts
for it, note it in Known gaps, and say so plainly when you hand the kit over. The board simply
drops the page. An invented voice is a fact you made up about someone's brand, and it will be
repeated in every piece of copy the kit ever writes.
