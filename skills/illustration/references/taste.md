# Illustration taste

What strong illustration looks like, in the editorial and brand work design publications feature. It
describes; it never decides: the brand's direction and its style block decide the look. A brand's
character has its own page, `references/taste.md` of the `character` skill, and artwork for print
its own, `references/taste.md` of the `artwork` skill.

## Strong

- **One idea per picture,** readable at phone size: the drawing says something a caption can't.
- **A clear gesture:** every figure's action reads from its silhouette alone.
- **A world of its own:** the place, the objects and the people all drawn with the same hand, the
  same line and the same few colours.
- **Shapes before detail:** big confident shapes, then detail only where the eye should land.
- **Space kept on purpose** for the words, as part of the composition: a sky, a wall, a table top.
- **People of the brand's world:** varied ages, builds and faces, in their own clothes, doing the
  brand's real work.

## Weak, and why it happens

- **Figures standing in a row, facing out:** no action was planned. Give each one a task.
- **Tiny heads, huge limbs and floating objects in every picture:** the model's stock "flat
  corporate" people. Name the brand's own style and real proportions.
- **Sparkles, hearts and stars filling the gaps:** the composition left space unplanned. Plan the
  empty space as a thing (a sky, a wall).
- **The same smile on every face:** no expression was planned. Give each figure the one the moment
  has.
- **A gesture that doesn't point at anything:** a gesture added to show an idea ("teaching",
  "explaining") instead of the action that does it. Pose the real action.

## Defaults to recognise

The image model's default figure is young, smiling and front-on, centred in an empty space with
scattered decoration. When the picture comes out like that, the plan left the figure, the action and
the composition open: name who they are, what they do and where they are in the frame.
