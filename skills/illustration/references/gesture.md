# Gesture and pose

Read this for step 4 of the plan. An illustrator draws the gesture before the body: the one line the
action makes through the figure, then the parts hung on it. Pose each figure in this order, one short
clause each.

## Each figure

- **Who:** their age range, build, gender and what they wear, or the animal's kind and size, plainly.
  Whoever checks the image holds it against this, so leave nothing about the figure to be guessed.
- **The action:** one. A second action at the same time splits the gesture and the model draws
  neither well; pick one.
- **The line of action:** how the body bends to do it ("leaning forward over the bench", "arched
  back to reach the top shelf"), so the pose reads from across a room.
- **The weight:** what carries the body (a foot, both knees, a chair, a hip on a wall); the body
  leans from it.
- **Each hand or paw:** its job and what it touches, with the grip. A hand with no job rests on
  something, hangs at the side or is out of frame. Two hands with two jobs are apart; they meet only
  when they work on one thing together.
- **The feet or paws:** where each rests, when they're in frame ("all four paws on the path").
- **The head:** where it turns and what the eyes look at.

**Sides.** Say which hand or leg only when the task decides it (a guitar's fretting hand, a
right-handed tool); then name it by the body and by the frame. Otherwise leave it to the model.

**Several figures.** Where each stands relative to the others, who touches whom and where (one hand
on one place), who looks at whom, and how they differ (age, size, colour, clothing). Keep their
silhouettes apart unless they touch on purpose.

## The brand's character

Its body, face and colours come from its character sheet and its Character lines, never from the
plan. The plan gives only this scene's action, gesture, head turn and expression, posed as above. A
prop it holds is one the scene needs, held as a character with its hands or paws could.

## Stylised bodies

A style may shorten limbs, drop fingers or round everything off, as the brand's style block says.
It never changes how a body works: the same number of limbs, each joined where it should be, a hand
the right way round for its arm, a foot for its leg.

## Poses image models get wrong

Use these only when the idea needs them, and then pose every hand:
- two hands working close together on one small thing;
- hands at or on the face;
- figures hugging or holding each other;
- a hand gripping a thin object (a pen, a handle, a string);
- a body twisting, the head one way and the shoulders another;
- an animal mid-leap or running, with all its legs in view.
