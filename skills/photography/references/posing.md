# Posing people

Read this for step 6 of the plan. A pose is decided from the moment: what the person is doing
decides where every part of them goes. Pose each person in this order, one short clause each.

## One person

- **Who:** their age range, gender and what they wear, plainly ("a man in his fifties in a canvas
  apron"). Whoever checks the image holds it against this, so leave nothing about the person to be
  guessed.
- **The action:** one, the moment's. A second action at the same time (drinking while reading a
  phone, waving while carrying a tray) splits the body between two tasks and the model draws neither
  well; pick one.
- **The weight:** what carries the body (both feet, one foot with the other leg loose, a chair, a
  hip against a counter, forearms on a table). A body leans from what carries it.
- **Each hand:** its job and what it touches, with the grip ("holds the cup by its handle", "rests
  flat on the desk beside the pen"). A hand with no job rests on something, hangs at the side, is in
  a pocket or is out of frame. Two hands with two jobs are apart; they meet only when they work on
  one thing together (both hands kneading, tying a knot).
- **The feet and legs:** where each foot is and what it stands on, when they're in frame.
- **The gaze:** at the task, at the other person or out of frame at something named. At the camera
  only in a portrait the shot is about.

**Sides.** Say which hand or leg only when the task decides it: a right-handed tool, a guitar's
fretting hand, the leg being treated, the hand nearer a table. Then name it two ways, by the body
and by the frame: "her right leg, the one nearer the camera". Otherwise leave the side to the model,
which picks a natural one.

## Two people or more

- **Where each is** relative to the other and to the camera ("across the counter from him", "behind
  her left shoulder"), and how far apart.
- **Who touches whom, and where:** "her hand on his shoulder", never "they embrace". A touch is one
  hand on one place.
- **Who looks where:** at each other, at the shared task, or one at the other.
- **Different in concrete ways:** age, build, hair, clothing, so the model draws two people, not one
  person twice.

## Poses image models get wrong

Use these only when the moment needs them, and then pose every hand and finger:
- two hands working close together on one small thing (threading, typing on a phone with both
  thumbs, interlaced fingers);
- hands at or on the face;
- people touching or holding each other;
- a hand gripping a thin object (a pen, a tool, a handlebar) in close-up;
- a body twisting (the head one way, the shoulders another).

For a hands close-up, one hand does the task and the other rests apart, or is out of frame.
