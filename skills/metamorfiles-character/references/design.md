# Writing the brief

Brief the character the way an art director briefs an illustrator: who, attitude, the thing it's
doing, the hand. Short and concrete. The model is better at inventing a face than a prompt is at
specifying one, so say what the character is like and let the drawing find the shapes; judge the
result by eye.

## What the brief says

- **Who it is**, as behaviour rather than an adjective: what it's doing and feeling right now.
- **The one thing** that makes it itself: a feature, a habit, a prop it's never without. Without it,
  every candidate is the category's mascot.
- **What makes it this brand's**: the detail from the business's own world it wears, holds or does.
- **Its attitude in the pose and the face**: a lean, a grin, a wink, a slurp, a run.
- **What it leaves out**: only what the brief or the category's codes rule out.

Not in the brief: a count of limbs, where each one goes, a colour per part, a list of bans against
clichés. That is an assembly diagram, and the model builds exactly what it reads: a thing standing
still with an emoji's face. Colours go in the prompt as the brand's palette with a job each
(`styles.md`), not a colour per limb.

## Two briefs for the same character

For Dew, the made-up mascot of Lumen, the example skincare brand:

Weak:
> A round water drop, wider at the bottom, its point curling at the top; two closed eyes drawn as
> curves and a small smile; two short arms ending in round mitts, no fingers, one raised; no legs.
> No sparkles, no bubbles, no sunglasses, no outline.

Strong:
> Dew is a sleepy water drop who loves slow mornings: leaning back mid-stretch, one little arm
> raised in a yawn, eyes closed in two happy curves, a tiny smile, its point curling over at the top
> like a bed-head curl. It is the drop of serum Lumen puts on your skin, and it never hurries.

The weak brief gets a drop with sticks for arms. The strong one leaves the shapes to the
illustrator and says what Dew is like.

## When a drawing misses

Change the brief, never describe the failed drawing back to the model, and draw again. Once a
drawing is kept, it is the anchor and then the sheet: from then on the identity lives in images, and
the words only say who it is and that nothing about it changes (`poses.md`).
