# Describing the character

The character comes from the brief and the chosen direction: who it is, how it carries itself, and
what makes it this brand's. Describe it the way an illustrator briefs themselves, in words, and let
the drawing find the shapes; judge the result by eye.

## What to describe

- **Who it is**, as behaviour rather than an adjective: what it's doing and feeling.
- **Its silhouette**: the parts that make its outline recognisable.
- **Its pose and gesture**, if it has one.
- **Its face**: what the eyes and mouth are doing.
- **What makes it this brand's**: the detail from the business's own world it wears or holds.
- **What it leaves out**, since image models add detail when they aren't told.

Hands and fingers are where drawings most often go wrong, and fine details vanish when the
character is small; say so in the description when they matter.

## Example

For Dew, the made-up mascot of Lumen, the example skincare brand:

> Dew is a sleepy water drop who loves slow mornings: a round, soft drop leaning back mid-stretch,
> one little arm raised in a yawn, eyes closed in two happy curves, a tiny smile, its point curling
> over at the top like a bed-head curl. Flat Clay with its face in Ink; no outline, no shading.

## When a drawing misses

Change the description, never describe the failed drawing back to the model, and draw again. A
character that changes between drawings needs its identity written once and repeated word for word
(`poses.md`).
