# Construction

A character people remember is built from a few simple shapes in clear proportions, with one feature
that makes it itself. Write its construction in shapes and numbers before drawing it: the image
model follows geometry far better than moods, and "cute" or "cheeky" alone gets the same generic
blob every time.

## The complexity budget

- **Three to seven basic shapes** make the whole figure: circles, ovals, rounded rectangles,
  triangles, drops. Merge or remove any shape that carries no identity or expression.
- **One defining feature**, and only one: a pair of long ears, a single tooth, a folded corner.
- **Two to four colours** in all, from the brand's palette, one of them for the features.
- **No detail smaller than a twentieth of its height.** It reads as a solid black silhouette, and its
  face reads at 32 px.

## The body

- **Name each shape and how they meet**: "the body is one tall rounded rectangle with a domed top; no
  separate head; two stubby legs, each a rounded rectangle a fifth of the body's height".
- **Proportions as ratios**: head to body (a big head, a third to a half of the height, reads young
  and friendly; a small one reads strong or odd), width to height, limb length against the body.
- **The silhouette first.** Its outline alone should say what it is: ears, horns, a tail or a shape
  that sticks out, on the sides and top where the outline shows them. Features inside the body don't
  change the silhouette.
- **Limbs simple**: tubes, stubs or mitten hands with no fingers. Fingers and toes are where drawings
  go wrong and small sizes lose them.
- **Weight against the lettering**: when it sits beside the name, its lines or its mass as heavy as
  the letters' ("its outline as thick as the stems of the letters in image 1").

## The face

The face carries the personality, so it is placed precisely:
- **Eyes**: their size as a share of the head's width (a tenth for small dots, a quarter for big
  round eyes), their spacing (wide-set reads friendly and young), their height (below the middle of
  the head reads younger).
- **Mouth**: one shape, sized and placed ("a small open D shape, half the eyes' spacing wide, just
  under them").
- **Two or three features at most**: eyes and a mouth, or eyes alone. No nose unless it is the
  defining feature, no eyebrows unless the expression needs them, no cheeks or highlights.
- **Expression through shape**: a wink as a short curved line, a grin as a wide half-disc, surprise
  as round eyes and an O.

## One defining feature, one asymmetry

- The **feature** is what someone would draw from memory: say what it is, its size against the body
  and where it sits.
- The **asymmetry** keeps it alive: one ear bent, one eye winking, the body leaning a little, one arm
  up. A perfectly symmetrical front view reads as clip-art.

## What is left out

Name it in the construction, since image models add detail when they aren't told: no outline around
flat fills, no shading or highlights, no texture, no fingers, no clothing unless it is the feature,
no props unless it is holding the brand's one object.

## Levels: full body, bust, head

Design it to work at three sizes: the full body for imagery and lockups, the bust for a profile
picture, the head alone for 16 to 32 px. The head must carry the defining feature and the
expression by itself; if it doesn't, the feature is in the wrong place.

## Writing it

Example, for Dew, the made-up mascot of Lumen, the example skincare brand:

> Dew is a water drop come alive: one drop shape, its point at the top leaning ten degrees to the
> right, as wide as it is two thirds tall; no separate head. Two small oval eyes, each a tenth of its
> width, set wide apart just below its middle, and one small closed smile between them, as wide as
> the eyes' spacing. Two short stubby arms, each a rounded tube a fifth of its height, one raised in
> a wave. Its defining feature is the leaning point, which curls into a small hook at its tip. One
> flat colour, Clay #c9785b, with the eyes and smile in Ink #1f1a17; no outline, no highlight, no
> shading, no fingers, no legs.

## Common failures

| It comes out | Change in the construction |
| --- | --- |
| A generic blob with a face stuck on | A silhouette with its feature sticking out; the face placed low and wide |
| Too many details (fur, buttons, patches) | The budget: name the shapes, and name what is left out |
| Stiff and symmetrical | One asymmetry: a lean, a tilted head, a raised arm |
| A different character in each drawing | The identity written once, repeated word for word (`poses.md`) |
| The face disappears at 32 px | Bigger eyes, fewer features, the head larger against the body |
| Scary when it should be friendly | Round shapes over pointed ones, eyes lower and wider, a closed smile |
