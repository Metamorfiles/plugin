# Principles of a studio-level identity

What separates identities that juries and critics single out from competent and generated ones,
drawn from the reference library (`library/`, 30 identities across every kind of business) and from
how the best studios work. Each principle has a test you can run on an image, and three levels
anchored in real work: **1** is what a generator or a template produces, **3** is competent and
interchangeable, **5** is studio work. Use them to make each step's options stronger before you show
them. They are never a score to show the user: the user's eye decides.

Each brief adds what is only true of it (`research.md`). These are the floor, not the ceiling.

## 1. One idea, taken from the business's own reality

A studio identity starts from one concrete thing the business owns, does or is: its name, material,
process, tools, place, people, archive, unit of measure, hours, habits. Everything else derives
from it, so a many-part system feels inevitable.

**Test:** say the idea in one sentence from the images alone, then point to the fact in the brief it
comes from.

- **1:** a mood ("warm and modern") or a category symbol; no idea to state.
- **3:** an idea stated in a caption or the story, visible only in the mark.
- **5:** the idea is in the construction and generates type, colour, imagery and applications.
  - `now-now`: each letter is a tiny room with a keyhole.
  - `fjord-sauna`: the degree sign replaces the O.
  - `castil`: the typeface is drawn as bent steel tube.
  - `pa-i-punt`: fermentation's cracks become torn forms.

## 2. A position on the category's codes

Every category has codes: colours, symbols, type, imagery. Studios map them, break most with a
reason, and keep the one that earns trust or helps a shopper.

**Test:** name the category's default look. Is it absent, or used knowingly and changed?

- **1:** the category kit: wheat for a bakery, a tooth for a dentist, a lotus for wellness,
  gradients for an app, navy and gold for a law firm.
- **3:** avoids the clichés but lands on a generic "modern" look any category could wear.
- **5:** a considered opposite with a reason, and the one trust code kept on purpose.
  - `the-modern-dentist`: lilac and plum instead of clinical white and blue.
  - `paist`: drops the claims and perfect teeth, keeps one colour per variant.
  - `hedeker-wealth-law`: keeps navy and a seal, wins on material.
  - `robinhood`: near-monochrome in rainbow fintech.

## 3. An asset only this brand could own

**Test (swap):** put a competitor's name on it; if it still works, it fails. **Test (unbranded):**
cover the name; one element still says which brand it is.

- **1:** the swap works; nothing is recognisable without the name.
- **3:** recognisable by combination (this colour with this font), not by one element.
- **5:** one element carries the brand alone: a colour, a glyph, a shape, a device.
  - `robinhood`: Robin Neon and the feather.
  - `fjord-sauna`: the degree sign.
  - `helsinki-city`: the wave edge.
  - `jolene`: the child's hand.

## 4. A mark with one idea and one anomaly

The wordmark is the default: the name is already the brand's. It earns character through one small
anomaly: one changed letter, one glyph, a full stop, a cut. A symbol needs a reason, and then it is
a member or container of the brand's own device family, simple enough to cut from paper, and still
itself at 16 px. A second reading may hide in the negative space, as a bonus, never a requirement.
Marks made from something real (a hand, a print, a tear) keep their imperfections; a drawn imitation
of them doesn't count.

**Test:** describe the mark after one look. Does it hold at 16 px, in one colour? Is its anomaly
tied to the idea?

- **1:**
  - a stock icon, or an initial in a circle or square;
  - a category shorthand (swoosh, leaf, spark);
  - a plain font with nothing of its own;
  - uneven geometry.
- **3:** clean and competent, but decorative: a nice shape that could belong to anyone.
- **5:** one anomaly carries the idea, and the mark belongs to the system.
  - `bugg`: the antenna g's.
  - `primer-schools`: the square full stop is the first block.
  - `mozilla`: the flag that becomes the T. rex.
  - `the-modern-dentist`: the logo is one of the tooth windows.

## 5. One expressive element, one disciplined partner

Studios spend boldness in one place and pair it with something plain, and they say which parts never
move and which are free.

**Test:** point to the one loud thing. Is everything else quieter and exact?

- **1:** everything competes (several effects, fonts, colours), or everything is equally bland.
- **3:** a hierarchy, but the expressive part is timid or the quiet part is sloppy.
- **5:** a clear loud element held by a rigid partner.
  - `jolene`: the scrawl with Karla.
  - `nuits-sonores`: the particles with a monoline grotesk.
  - `caffe-nazionale`: the hand-painting with Helvetica.
  - `mailchimp`: the calm core with the surreal layer.

## 6. A device that is the mark's own grammar

The device (pattern, frame, crop, rule) comes from the mark or the idea, and the logo and the pattern
are the same object at different scales. A mark that is a container gives variety for free.

**Test:** is the device derived from the mark or the idea, and is it reused as a unit, frame or crop
across applications?

- **1:** decoration unrelated to the mark (random shapes, stock patterns), or no device at all.
- **3:** a device that fits the palette but could be swapped for another.
- **5:** the mark's geometry organises everything.
  - `helsinki-city`: the crest's frame and waves.
  - `john-lewis-partnership`: the bars.
  - `museu-nacional-da-republica`: the portal.
  - `fjord-sauna`: the degree sign as a pixel.

## 7. Colour: one owned signal, rationed and named

One colour does the recognising and is used sparingly; the rest is black, white or neutrals cast from
it. Colours are named after the business's world, with shares, allowed pairs and what they are never
for.

**Test:** is there a lead colour and a clear proportion? Where do the colours come from? Is the
category's colour code avoided or used knowingly?

- **1:**
  - five or six equal swatches;
  - the category's default palette;
  - AI defaults: cream, serif and terracotta; indigo-violet; navy and electric blue; beige and
    sage; black and acid green.
- **3:** a tasteful palette with no signal colour or no reason.
- **5:** a named signal colour from the business's own world, rationed.
  - `castil`: the RAL powder-coat chart.
  - `burger-king`: colours named after ingredients.
  - `nuits-sonores`: one green that the audience adopted.
  - `robinhood`: one neon on black and greys.

## 8. Type with a job and a feature

One or two faces, each with a job. The characterful part has one distinctive feature tied to the
idea and is made typeable so it outlives the launch. Not a stock default.

**Test:** why this face, in one sentence? What is its one feature? Could it be Inter, Montserrat or
Poppins without loss?

- **1:** a default sans, or a default sans-and-serif pair, with no reason; a wordmark that is a
  stock face set untouched.
- **3:** a good face well set, but chosen for mood only; a wordmark with nothing drawn of its own.
- **5:** a face with a job and a feature tied to the idea.
  - `castil`: the mono is drawn as tube.
  - `fjord-sauna`: the ° is the one changed glyph.
  - `burger-king`: a face you want to bite.
  - `barrault-pressacco`: one size is the whole system.

## 9. Imagery with one strict, honest rule

Imagery follows one rule anyone can apply, and it works without a logo. Real people, places and
materials, never stock.

**Test:** state the imagery rule. Would an image following it be recognisable without the logo?

- **1:** stock or generic generated mood (a desk, a latte, a smiling model), with no rule.
- **3:** a consistent look (a filter, a palette) without a rule of its own.
- **5:** one rule, visible in every image.
  - `robinhood`: one object, one light, one metaphor.
  - `six-six`: the marque masks the eyes.
  - `bugg`: one bug per product.
  - `primer-schools`: real students, no models.

## 10. Words that only this brand could say

Voice is written as lines, verbs and limits, and the lines do visual work: they become posters,
signs and packaging. Practical information set as a headline is voice too.

**Test:** could a competitor say these lines unchanged?

- **1:** a generic tagline ("Crafted with love", "Build faster") or none.
- **3:** a pleasant line that fits the category.
- **5:** lines only this brand could say, used as material.
  - `museu-nacional-da-republica`: SEU MUSEU.
  - `graza`: Sizzle and Drizzle.
  - `now-now`: Not Right Now.
  - `caffe-nazionale`: the dialect motto.
  - `fjord-sauna`: the temperature is the message.

## 11. The business's own touchpoints, each using the system differently

Studios design the humble, frequent surfaces of this business: the sugar sachet, the spec tag, the
invoice, the receipt, the door, the app icon at 60 px. Each uses a different part of the system: the
mark cropped big, a pattern, a colour flood, a small wordmark. The space itself carries the device.

A system proves itself by flexing: three products, editions or services that change by one rule
and still read as one brand.

**Test:**
- Are these the objects this business really uses?
- Does each use the system in its own way?
- Is there a set that varies by a rule?
- Are they finished, at real proportions, or empty frames?

- **1:** the stock mockup set (business card, tote, phone) with the logo pasted in the centre of
  each, or a few empty outlines.
- **3:** real touchpoints, but the same lockup placed on each, few of them, or thinly finished.
- **5:** many humble, specific surfaces, finished, each a different move of the system, and a
  variation set.
  - `castil`: every product gets its RAL colour band on the same notched tag.
  - `robinhood`: the Gold tier changes neon to metal, nothing else.
  - `castil`: the spec tags.
  - `caffe-nazionale`: the sugar sachets and crockery.
  - `the-modern-dentist`: tooth arches as doors.
  - `bikedot`: hand-rolled newspapers.

## 12. A system someone else can run

A few explicit rules let the owner, staff or another designer keep going: layers, one size, a kit of
parts, a tool.

**Test:** can you write the system in three rules from the images?

- **1:** no rules; each piece is its own design.
- **3:** consistency by habit, not by rule.
- **5:** three rules or fewer explain everything shown.
  - `pa-i-punt`: ground, type, disruption.
  - `barrault-pressacco`: one face, one size.
  - `urban-climb`: a fixed frame plus loose parts.
  - `helsinki-city`: one logo and one wave per view.

## 13. Craft that looks labored over

**Grid and spacing**
- One grid; equal margins.
- Nothing overlapping or crowding an edge.

**Type**
- Few sizes, with dramatic scale contrast.
- Display tracking tightened.

**The mark**
- Optical corrections: circles overshoot, reversed marks thinned.
- The lockup at a deliberate ratio.

**Presentation**
- Shown as if it already exists, in use.

**Test:** at full size, is anything uneven, crowded, mismatched or accidental?

- **1:** misaligned, crowded or clip-art: mixed perspectives, outlines, generic shadows, placeholder
  text.
- **3:** tidy but even-handed: every element the same size, everything centred.
- **5:** exact and confident.
  - dramatic scale;
  - tight gutters;
  - one hero;
  - details that reward close reading (the nibbled tags in `bugg`, the upside-down label in `paist`).

## 14. Fit

Everything suits this product and this audience: colour, type, imagery and voice feel like what is
being sold and to whom.

**Test:** show it to the audience without the name. Do they feel the product?

- **1:** it contradicts the product: a tech look for soft bedding, a spa look for a gym.
- **3:** neutral: it could suit this business or many others.
- **5:** every choice makes the product more itself.
  - `graza`: the squeeze bottle's use-it energy.
  - `six-six`: a yellow chosen to flatter skin at the eyewear counter.
  - `fjord-sauna`: cold black with one warm burgundy.

## Levels, and what they mean

An option reaches studio level when principles 1, 3, 4, 11 and 14 score 5 and nothing scores below
3.
One 1 anywhere is a failure: fix it before anyone sees it. An average around 3 is exactly the
competent, interchangeable work a generator can already do, and it is not good enough.
