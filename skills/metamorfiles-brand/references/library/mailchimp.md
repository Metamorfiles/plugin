# Mailchimp

- **Name / business / place / year / studio:** Mailchimp, email and marketing platform for small and midsize businesses (acquired by Intuit in 2021 for about $12B). Atlanta, Georgia, USA. Launched 27 September 2018. Identity by COLLINS (New York / San Francisco; team incl. Brian Collins' studio, Tom Elia, Matt Luckhurst) with Mailchimp's in-house brand and creative team; R/GA partnered on strategy/product. Illustrations by in-house illustrators and commissioned artists worldwide (e.g. Amber Vittoria, per Web Designer Depot). Later custom serif "Means" developed with type designer Greg Gazdowicz (per Canny Creative's atlas; unverified at source).

## Brief & business truth
Mailchimp was the beloved, quirky email tool of small businesses, but it had grown into a marketing platform (landing pages, ads, postcards, CRM, dozens of acquired and built tools) and was still seen as "just email". The brand was fragmented across 35 offerings, and the SaaS category around it was converging on flat blue, geometric sans and generic vector people. It needed credibility for a bigger promise without losing the weirdness and humour that made founders love it. Its founders' identity: "a little guy fighting for the little guys".

## Strategy / insight
Small businesses are run by creative, unconventional people who don't want to look like everyone else; Mailchimp should democratise sophisticated marketing technology for them and celebrate their individuality. Collins framed the design idea as purposeful, playful surrealism: grounded, consistent structure for trust; expressive, odd, hand-made creativity for personality. The portfolio was reorganised from 35 solutions into five categories.

## The idea (one sentence)
Serious tools, sincerely weird: a sunny, stable frame (Cavendish Yellow, Freddie, a warm 1920s serif) holds a world of surreal hand-drawn creatures that stands for the creativity of the small businesses Mailchimp serves.
**Source:** people (the unconventional small-business owner, and Mailchimp's own "little guy" culture) plus heritage (Freddie, the brand's long-standing humour).

## Register
Editorial-illustrative / character world: warm serif editorial pages populated by outsider-art ink drawings, on one signature yellow.

## Category codes it used or broke
- Broke: SaaS blue, geometric sans, "corporate Memphis" flat vector people, tidy isometric illustrations. Replaced with yellow, a Cooper serif and raw ink illustration.
- Broke: the trend of oversimplifying heritage mascots into abstract icons; Freddie survives, simplified but recognisably a character.
- Used: SaaS product clarity (clean grotesk in product UI, pale functional backgrounds), so the weirdness never gets in the way of use.

## Mark
- **Type:** character symbol (Freddie) + lowercase wordmark; Freddie alone as icon/avatar.
- **Freddie construction:** a chimp head in three-quarter view facing right, wearing a postman's cap (brim to the upper right). Solid black silhouette with the face as a single yellow/negative mask shape: a large muzzle and grin, one eye winking (a curved stroke), the other a dot, two small nostril dots. A round ear with a spiral inner line on the left. The cap is drawn in outline (black line, negative interior) with the brim as a thick black shape. Compared to the previous Freddie: fewer fur tufts, the old "M" in the hair removed, a simplified ear, heavier and more even strokes so it holds at tiny sizes (per Web Designer Depot).
- **Optical details:** the wink carries the personality; the cap is the only outline element, keeping the head readable as a solid mass.
- **Variants:** black on Cavendish Yellow (hero), black on light grey, reversed white on Peppercorn/black; since 2021 an "Intuit mailchimp" endorsed lockup with INTUIT set in caps above the wordmark.

## Wordmark / lettering
Custom lowercase "mailchimp" (the C deliberately lowercased, signalling more than email), replacing Jessica Hische's 2013 script. Chunky, heavy, slightly off-kilter, retro sans with a Cooper-ish softness: stubby rounded terminals, slightly flared stroke ends, uneven rhythm (the l and h ascenders stand tall, the p descends deep), tight spacing. Set to the right of Freddie, its x-height about half Freddie's height, vertically centred on the head.

## Type system
- **Cooper Light** (1920s Oswald Cooper design, chosen for a sincere, trustworthy, warm personality): headlines and marketing statements, set large, tight, in title or sentence case, often ragged centred on web pages and flush-left stacked on posters. Later evolved into the custom Cooper-derived serif "Means".
- **Graphik**-style grotesk for body copy and product UI (named by Canny Creative; the 2018 guideline pages show a neutral grotesk in body text).
- Type-as-image: very large Cooper headlines interleaved with small illustrations in the gaps between words (poster: "Growing Up Doesn't Have To Mean Buttoning Up.").

## Colour system (published)
- **Cavendish Yellow** #FFE01B: the hero colour, "sunshine and optimism"; guidelines say to anchor on this one colour, used with purpose, for recognition across all properties.
- **Peppercorn** #241C15: near-black accent; text, Freddie, line work.
- Functional colours: #241C15, #403B3B, #DBD9D2, #EFEEEA, teal #007C89.
- Background colours (pale tints): #D8EACC (sage), #C5DBF2 (sky), #FBEECA (cream), #F9E0FA (blush).
- Accent colours (hex read from a small guideline image; approximate): pink ≈ #FF3EBF, plum #692340, ochre #E7B75F, green ≈ #00C14E, orange ≈ #F25F25; stated as the flexible palette for merch, stickers, t-shirts.
- Proportions: yellow as full-field hero moments; pale tints as page backgrounds for product marketing; illustrations black ink with yellow as the only spot colour.

## Device(s) / graphic language
- **Ink illustration library:** black brush-and-pen drawings (loose, textural, sometimes scratchy), surreal subjects: creatures with eyes, sentient mushrooms, hands, birds with odd bodies, plants, cars on too many wheels, people with long flowing hair. Yellow applied as a flat spot fill on one or two elements.
- **Animation:** creatures and objects that are free but observed, familiar but abstracted; motion used to reveal surprise.
- **Brand principles as a pair:** "Consistent and grounded" (logo, palette, type) vs "Playful and expressive" (illustration, animation, photography).
- **Surreal staging in photography:** products placed in nature (a monitor in a jungle with a toucan, a phone inside a cactus).

## Imagery
Two strands. Illustration: outsider-art inspired, personal expression over realism, never cartoon-slick, drawn by in-house and commissioned artists so the library is varied in hand but unified by black ink + yellow. Photography: authentic, inclusive portraits of real customers in their own shops and studios, natural colour, environmental context; plus surreal still-life/nature staging for product.

## Voice
From the Content Style Guide: plainspoken but not hyperbolic; genuine but not patronising; a translator but not condescending; dry humour but not inappropriate (closer to a wink than a shout). Tone shifts with the reader's state; clarity beats entertainment; active voice, plain English, positive framing. Freddie never speaks; no writing in character. Sample lines: "Your business was born for this." "Growing up doesn't have to mean buttoning up." "Create better campaigns with Mailchimp."

## Applications
Website and product marketing pages, product UI (loading and error states with Freddie), posters and OOH, editorial magazine spreads, social, merch and stickers, office environment, Postcards product launch (2018), and the post-Intuit endorsed lockups.
What varies: illustration artist and subject, background tint, yellow full-field vs spot, photography type. What stays fixed: Freddie, the lowercase wordmark, Cavendish Yellow, Cooper serif headlines, black ink line.

## Guidelines structure
Public: brand assets (logo files, naming "Mailchimp" with lowercase c, colours Cavendish Yellow and Peppercorn, clear-space and misuse rules), Content Style Guide (voice and tone, grammar, writing for accessibility, etc.). 2018 launch guideline pages (shown in press) cover: colour (hero, functional, background, accent), typography, illustration, animation, photography (authentic and inclusive portraits), principles (consistent and grounded / playful and expressive).

## What makes it ownable
Cavendish Yellow; the winking Freddie with postman's cap; Cooper serif in SaaS; surreal black-ink creatures with one yellow spot.

## What a generic version would have been
A blue-purple gradient, an abstract envelope or "M" monogram replacing the monkey, a geometric sans wordmark, and flat vector people with oversized limbs pointing at dashboards.

## Principles it demonstrates
- Split the system explicitly into a grounded core (logo, colour, type) and an expressive layer (illustration, motion), and name both as principles.
- Own one colour so completely that a field of it is the brand, and keep everything else subordinate.
- Keep and simplify a heritage character rather than abstract it away; the wink is worth more than a new geometric icon.
- Use a warm vintage serif to signal sincerity in a category of cold sans.
- Commission many hands but unify them with strict material rules (black ink, one yellow spot, surreal subject).
- Write voice as paired traits with limits ("X, but not Y"), and decide whether the mascot speaks.
