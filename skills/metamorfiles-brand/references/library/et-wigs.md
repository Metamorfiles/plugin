# E.T WIGS

- **Name / business / place / year / studio:** E.T WIGS, a Black-owned luxury wig brand and wig store (buy, rent and custom units, plus hair products such as edge control and glue remover), founded by wig maker Endah Tche, whose work has appeared in Vogue, Wonderland and Dazed. London, UK. Rebrand published late 2024 (site dated 2025). PROST WORK / Prost Studio (London and Marseille; designer Manon Prost): art direction, visual identity, packaging, web design. Featured by The Brand Identity, Mindsparkle Mag and It's Nice That (studio profile, Feb 2025).

## Brief & business truth
Tche assisted stylists in London and Paris for two years before founding the brand, inspired by her peers and other Black women in the world of wigs. Audience: young, style-led Black women (and anyone) who treat hair as expression. Wig brands tend to look overly girly and predictable (pink, script, glam). The brand's belief is that change is power; wigs let you become someone new.

## Strategy / insight
A wig is transformation you can put on. Everything centres on becoming "The Next You": not a promise, an invitation to experiment. Aesthetic concept "Extra Sleek": eccentric audacity balanced by clean sophistication, with sci-fi and retro-futurist references (the initials E.T. read as extraterrestrial as well as the founder's name).

## The idea (one sentence) and its source
Metamorphosis: the brand is a butterfly moment, the instant you step out as the next you, told through a liquid E-T monogram that mirrors into a butterfly, an alien star, and chrome. **Source:** moment (transformation), reinforced by the founder's initials.

## Register
Loud display, retro-futurist luxury: high-contrast serif at poster scale, chrome and lavender on black.

## Category codes it used or broke
- Broke: bubblegum pink, script "glam" logos, hair-flip beauty-salon stock photography, influencer-style promo graphics.
- Used and exaggerated: hair itself as the hero image (full-frame waves), the before/after idea turned into a brand-wide theme of transformation; the butterfly (a common beauty cliché) redrawn so abstractly it reads first as letters.

## Mark
- **Wordmark:** "E.T✶WIGS" in capitals, a customised version of Swear (OH no Type Company). Very high stroke contrast, hairline serifs flaring into sharp wedge points, tall narrow proportions. "E.T" is roman; "WIGS" leans (a display italic/oblique cut) and the W's strokes cross and split, giving speed. The full stop after T is a large round dot sitting on the baseline.
- **The star:** a thin six-pointed asterisk-like star replaces the space between E.T and WIGS, sitting around cap-middle height; its spikes are long hairlines tapering to points, sized close to the x-height of the caps' serifs. It "breaks" the wordmark and adds the spacey note; it is also used alone as punctuation (the ☆ between words on jar lids).
- **Monogram:** a fluid calligraphic figure where an E and a T are drawn as one liquid ribbon; the form is mirrored left to right so it becomes a symmetrical butterfly (upper and lower wings as loops) and also suggests a W. The top ends terminate in teardrop drips, like antennae. Rendered as chrome or glossy liquid purple (embossed on jar lids), or flat lavender on black boxes.
- Variants: horizontal wordmark; wordmark stacked on two lines (E.T / WIGS) on box spines; monogram alone; wordmark in outline (hollow strokes) at huge scale on dark grounds.

## Wordmark / lettering
Capitals with dots and star as structural glyphs; tight letterspacing at display sizes, letters nearly touching. Crops happily off the page edge.

## Type system
- Swear (custom), display: wordmark, headlines ("Break free from the ordinary", "Try our wigs & spread your wings"), tagline. Mixes roman and italic inside one line to stress a word: "the *next* you".
- Inter Tight: body copy, specs, navigation, jar ring text (uppercase, tracked "EDGE CONTROL ☆ ETWIGS.CO.UK" set on a circle around the lid).
- Headlines in sentence case, tight leading; body justified or ragged in small sizes.

## Colour system
Black, purple/lavender, silver (chrome), white (The Brand Identity). Values sampled from published images (inferred):
- Near-black (boxes, backgrounds), about #261F24, slightly warm.
- Lavender (print fields, merch), about #DBB9FF; softer lilac on jars about #CFB9D8.
- Deeper violet for glossy monogram, about #B692CB and darker.
- Chrome/silver as a finish, not a flat colour.
- Pairings: lavender on black; black on lavender; white wordmark over photos. No pink.

## Device(s) / graphic language
1. **The star** as separator and sparkle, also in ring text on lids.
2. **Chrome and liquid rendering** of the monogram: glossy, dimensional, as if poured.
3. **Transformation imagery treatment:** photos inverted into negative and toned lavender on black (a body turned alien), satin and faux-fur textures in purple as grounds.
4. **Wordmark at extreme scale**, cropped, outlined, repeated on box tops and page edges.
5. **"The next you" stencil:** the tagline spray-stencilled in lavender on pavement, a street-level teaser.
6. **Mirror:** a real mirror poster with the headline printed on it, so the viewer sees themselves as "the next you".

## Imagery
Editorial fashion photography from a roster of photographers (all using E.T products): tight crops on hair as material (copper waves, platinum, two-tone), backs and profiles, hair in motion, duo portraits in blue-violet light, a black-and-white back view with metal clips. Grades: cool, violet, high contrast; some negative/inverted treatments. Never the glossy salon before/after, never stock smiles.

## Voice
Confident, cheeky, empowering: "Break free from the ordinary", "Try our wigs & spread your wings", "Don't make me take my wig off" (on a tank top), "the next you". Rules (inferred): short, first- and second-person, playful threats and dares; wordplay on wig/wings; never apologetic.

## Applications
Black shipping and product boxes (lavender print, wordmark and monogram), edge-control jars with embossed monogram and ring type, glue-remover packaging, business cards on satin, thank-you cards and envelopes, lookbook binder, merch (tank top), mirror poster, pavement stencils, website (full-bleed hair hero with small wordmark), Instagram grid, phone UI.
- **Fixed:** wordmark with star, monogram, lavender/black/chrome, Swear + Inter Tight.
- **Varies:** scale and crop of the wordmark, finish of the monogram (flat, embossed, liquid chrome), photographic treatment (natural, inverted, violet-lit), copy line.

## Guidelines structure
None published.

## What makes it ownable
The star inside the name; the butterfly-shaped liquid E-T monogram; lavender on black; the "next you" line with italic "next".

## What a generic version would have been
A pink-and-gold script logotype, a silhouette of a woman with flowing hair, glossy salon photos, and "luxury hair" set in thin spaced capitals.

## Principles it demonstrates
- Choose the brand's one moment (transformation) and make every device depict change: mirroring, inversion, liquid metal.
- Let an existing cliché (the butterfly) in only if it is drawn so abstractly it reads as your letters first.
- Mix roman and italic of one display serif to create voice inside a single line.
- Use a single glyph (a star) as a signature that can live alone as punctuation.
- Swap the category colour (pink) for a neighbour with more edge (lavender on black) to stay in beauty but out of the cliché.
- Put the viewer in the brand (a mirror poster) when the promise is about who they become.
