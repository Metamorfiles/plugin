# Urban Climb

- **Name / business / place / year / studio**
  Urban Climb, a chain of bouldering gyms in Australian inner cities (Melbourne: Collingwood, Blackburn, Thornbury; Brisbane: Milton, West End; Adelaide). Identity by Base Design (Melbourne office; creative and art direction and design by Caroline Cox and Daniel Peterson), with brand strategy by Untangld (Danish Chan). Work dated about 2021-2022 (Boulderfest 2021 poster; Untangld lists a 2022 AGDA Distinction); press coverage February 2023. Folio photography Annika Kafcaloudis; Boulderfest 2021 illustration Jiro Bevis.

- **Brief & business truth:**
  One of Australia's largest and fastest-growing climbing gym operators, about to triple in size within 18 months as competition rose and bouldering went mainstream after its Olympic debut. Its gyms sit in city centres, not suburban sheds, so the members are a mixed, creative crowd, and the business ran on a cult community. The problem: scale up without gentrifying or diluting that close-knit culture, and stay credible to die-hards while welcoming complete beginners.

- **Strategy / insight:**
  Untangld: ignore the category's fixation on climbing clichés (peaks, carabiners, heroic summits) and centre the brand on the mix of people drawn to the challenge. Base: treat bouldering as a subculture, not a sport, and write that down as a manifesto ("Our Uncommon Ground") before designing anything. BP&O read it as a strong core carrying deliberately loose exterior signifiers, a business that puts "culture first, colour second."

- **The idea (one sentence)** and **its source**
  A brand built like a bouldering wall: a fixed frame of rules plus a kit of loose parts (stickers, hand lettering, slogans) that staff keep re-setting, because in bouldering there is never one way up. Source: people (the bouldering subculture, its in-jokes and its "any route" ethic).

- **Register:** loud DIY / subculture vernacular: sticker-bomb, hand-marker lettering, skate-and-zine attitude, held in place by a plain condensed grotesk and black-and-cream basics.

- **Category codes it used or broke,** and how
  - Broke: mountain silhouettes, carabiner and rope icons, performance-gym gradients, aspirational athlete photography, "push your limits" copy. Holds and knots appear, but as found objects and doodles, not heroic symbols.
  - Broke: the single rigid master brand. Every touchpoint is allowed its own treatment; unused identity concepts from the pitch were recycled as staff uniform and merch graphics instead of being discarded.
  - Used, with a wink: climbing jargon ("problems", "routes", "send") and gym realities (bruises, chalk, drills, falling), turned into slogans that insiders get and newcomers can still decode.

- **Mark:**
  - Primary: a wordmark, URBAN CLIMB. with a closing full stop (see Wordmark). It runs horizontally, stacked on two lines (URBAN / CLIMB., flush left), and vertically, rotated 90 degrees to read bottom-to-top up a building's height, the orientation that mirrors climbing (BP&O singles out the logo's vertical structure).
  - BP&O describes the logo's dotted grid as the grid of bolt holes on a climbing wall, standing for "any route" (any hold can go in any hole). In the images saved, the dot field appears as a regular bolt-hole grid behind the Boulderfest poster art; a standalone construction drawing of logo plus grid was not published in any source I could read (inferred: the grid is a background or holding field the wordmark and stickers sit on, not a part drawn into the letters).
  - Secondary: a "UC" monogram in the hand-marker face, set inside a circular seal whose ring carries URBAN CLIMB. and UNITED BY UNCOMMON GROUND. in a wide, thin geometric sans (embroidered tonally on merch). A smiley made from a rotated "UC" (U as a smile, C forms) serves as a sticker and cap badge (inferred reading from the cap and tote images).
  - Small sizes: the UC monogram and the round sticker/patch shape.

- **Wordmark / lettering:**
  All caps, a condensed medium-weight grotesk with slightly soft, hand-cut edges and tight tracking, ending in a full stop that works like a flat statement. The site's heading face is a custom cut named Super Grotesk UC ALT Condensed Medium (the "UC ALT" in the file name suggests alternates drawn or modified for Urban Climb (inferred)). The same letters are painted huge on brick and shutter facades.

- **Type system:** (names from urbanclimb.com.au CSS)
  - Super Grotesk UC ALT Condensed Medium (headings h1 to h5): all caps, tight leading (about 0.9), slight negative tracking on h2 (-0.02em). The workhorse: hours, gym names, slogans, the wordmark.
  - Quick Marker UC (custom version of a marker script; "alt-font" headings): a brushy, hand-drawn all-caps marker with bouncy baselines, used for slogans, sticker text and merch ("JOIN TODAY", "INSIDE", "NO BAD CLIMBS"). It is the handmade voice set as a font, so staff can produce it.
  - ABC Marfa (Dinamo) for body copy and ABC Marfa Mono for captions and small labels: calm, neutral text that stays out of the way.
  - A wide, thin geometric sans on the seal and neon (inferred to be Marfa or a related wide face).
  - Type-as-image moves: one word set diagonally in marker between two lines of grotesk (OUTSIDERS / INSIDE / TOGETHER), the same slogan repeated eight times in a solid block (CLIMB WEIRD), marker words wrapped around a hand-drawn thought cloud, headlines rotated vertical to climb a wall, marker slogans printed in a wave across a tee.

- **Colour system:**
  - Core: black (#000000, with #131313 and #242424 as UI darks), white, and a warm off-white page (#EFEBE4). Nearly every application is one colour on one ground.
  - Accent: a hot orange (#F36C0E on the site, as text selection; a similar orange in Boulderfest print and the countdown post). Red #F41F0C for errors.
  - Per-gym pastels (the site gives each gym a colour): #C7D8DD pale blue-grey, #DCFFEC mint, #FFFFC8 and #FEFFDD pale yellow, #FCF0CF cream, #F0FFE6 and #F5FFDF pale greens, #E7FFFF pale cyan; a magenta #D524E3 for the offer starburst.
  - Merch and campaigns take loose, climbing-gear colours: mustard, maroon, washed charcoal with cyan print, acid yellow halftone. Guidance was published as "colours" plus freedom; no fixed ratios were published. Colour is intentionally secondary; recognition comes from type and voice.

- **Device(s) / graphic language:**
  - Stickers: a set of illustrated stickers (a running brain with a drill, googly eyes, a coffin marked "old routes", a screaming mouth, a smiley UC, a chalk bottle) that are layered over each other and over photos, walls and bags. BP&O reads them as holding each other in place through tension, like a climber on a wall. Rule (inferred): stickers overlap and crowd; never a single tidy icon in a corner.
  - Bolt-hole dot grid: a regular dot field from the wall's T-nut holes, used as a background field and a route metaphor.
  - Hand-drawn objects in bold black outline with a white fill: a figure-eight knot painted about three metres tall on a roller door; the thought cloud; a drill.
  - Halftone: photos printed as coarse, one-colour halftone dots (black on acid yellow) as poster grounds.
  - Vertical setting: wordmark and slogans rotated to read upward on neon, facades and posters.
  - Boulderfest: an annual illustrator commission, reinventing the event's theme each year (2021: Jiro Bevis, orange, black and halftone on mint, with the dot grid and a zig-zag route line).

- **Imagery:**
  Warm, close, slightly grainy editorial photography by Annika Kafcaloudis: real climbers mid-move, shot from above or behind, with dusty chalked holds, plywood wall panels and bolt holes in frame. Bright plastic holds give the colour; the palette of the photos is the gym's own. Architecture shot in desaturated black and white. What never appears (inferred): mountain vistas, summit-hero poses, slick studio fitness shots, stock smiles.

- **Voice:**
  Manifesto "Our Uncommon Ground", twelve short lines, e.g. This place was made for you; Outsiders, inside, together; Open to, but not for everyone; Take climbing seriously, not yourself; There's never one way; Fitness isn't a beauty contest; May cause bruises and happiness; No bad climbs; Always be uprising.
  Rules drawn from them: short declarative lines, often ending in a full stop; paradox and contradiction pairs (open to / not for; seriously / not yourself); insider puns that newcomers can still decode ("Nothing But Problems", where a problem is a boulder route; "old routes" on a coffin; "Climb weird"); honest about pain and failure; no performance boasting. Sign-off line: United by uncommon ground. Word swaps: problems not challenges, climbers not athletes, community not members (inferred).

- **Applications:**
  Neon sign reading NOTHING BUT PROBLEMS with a down arrow, set vertically beside a vertical painted wordmark; black facades with white painted type (hours plus stacked wordmark) and a giant knot mural; posters; staff uniforms and merch (tees, crewnecks, bucket hats, caps, totes, embroidered patches) built from recycled unused concepts; stickers; Boulderfest posters and tees; social countdown posts (huge orange numerals, googly-eye sticker, small caps line); website with per-gym colour cards.
  What varies: which sticker, which slogan, the colour, the layout, the illustrator each year, whether a line is marker or grotesk. What stays fixed: the condensed caps grotesk and its full stop, the marker face, black and white as base, the manifesto lines as the copy bank, the vertical gesture.

- **Guidelines structure:** No guidelines published. Base lists the kit it handed over: logo, illustrated sticker set, colours, typefaces, image treatments, tone of voice principles, with the explicit aim that the in-house team keeps iterating.

- **What makes it ownable:**
  The manifesto lines used as slogans; the pairing of a stiff condensed caps grotesk (with its full stop) against a bouncy marker hand; the crowded sticker world (brain with drill, googly eyes, coffin of old routes); vertical wordmarks climbing buildings; NOTHING BUT PROBLEMS.

- **What a generic version would have been:**
  A mountain-peak or carabiner symbol above a bold italic sans, a teal-and-orange "adventure" palette with gradient, hero photos of a chalked hand gripping a hold against a dark backdrop, and "Reach new heights" copy, applied identically at every gym.

- **Principles it demonstrates:**
  1. Write the culture down first (a manifesto of short, quotable lines), then use those lines as the brand's main visual asset.
  2. When the idea is "there's never one way", ship a kit of rules and loose parts, not a fixed layout, and let every touchpoint be different.
  3. Pair one disciplined typeface with one handmade face so the brand can be both legible and scrappy, and make the handmade voice a font the team can use.
  4. Take graphics from the activity's real objects (bolt-hole grid, knots, drills, chalk, holds), never from category clichés.
  5. Speak in insider jargon that outsiders can still decode, so the brand welcomes newcomers without losing credibility with regulars.
  6. Keep colour secondary and loose when voice and type carry recognition; a neutral black, white and cream base lets any accent join.
