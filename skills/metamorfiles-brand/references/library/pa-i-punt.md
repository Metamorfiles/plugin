# Pa i Punt

- **Name:** Pa i Punt (Catalan, literally "bread and dot/full stop"; read as "bread, full stop", nothing more to say) (inferred reading; not glossed by the studio)
- **Business:** Small artisanal bakery (stone-ground grains, sourdough, long fermentation) run by baker-founder Aniol; open since 2020
- **Place:** Sant Quirze del Vallès, near Barcelona, Catalonia, Spain
- **Year:** 2025 (published Sept 2025)
- **Studio:** N.O.V.A LAB, Barcelona (Enric Pérez)

## Sources

- N.O.V.A LAB case study: https://www.novalab.design/work/pa-i-punt
- The Brand Identity feature (Sept 2025): https://the-brandidentity.com/project/n.o.v.a-lab-turns-bread-making-imperfections-into-bold-bakery-branding
- No public guidelines. Press coverage is narrower than the other cases (TBI plus studio page); included for its craft-derived system and clear rules.

## Brief & business truth

A one-shop craft bakery whose founder is obsessive about process (grain, milling, fermentation time) and whose stance is political: good bread is a right, fast food is the enemy. The posters' manifesto line says, in Catalan, that the bakers are combative artisans tired of fast food and want everyone to have the right to decent, quality bread. The identity had to stand apart from cute, warm bakery branding and read as a statement, while staying simple enough for the baker to run himself across packaging, signage, merch, the shop and social.

## Strategy / insight

Pérez saw that bakery branding defaults to friendly, soft and comforting; this client needed something like a protest. The breakthrough came from watching the bread being made: the cracks, bubbles and counter-forms that fermentation and baking produce, the visible evidence of time and imperfection. Studio line: the imperfections were not a problem to solve but the raw material of the system.

## The idea

**One sentence:** The bread's own fermentation cracks and holes become torn, organic shapes that break into an otherwise strict typographic layout, like a protest poster made of dough.
**Source:** product-craft (the bread-making process and its imperfections), with the name's "full stop" as a verbal and graphic hook.

## Register

Loud display / protest poster; brutal, bold, experimental but systematic.

## Category codes it used or broke

- Broke: warm browns, script lettering, wheat illustrations, cute characters, kraft-and-twine rusticity.
- Broke: bakery softness. Uses the grammar of activist posters (big hours and phone numbers, manifesto quotes, coordinates) instead.
- Used (subverted): the wheat ear appears, but as a ragged torn-paper silhouette, not a pretty icon.

## Mark

- **Type:** custom wordmark with a built-in full stop.
- **Construction:** "PA i PUNT." in ultra-heavy, very wide, blobby rounded letterforms set with almost no space, so letters touch and fuse. P and A have circular counters like punched holes; A is a rounded arch; the lowercase i is a short stem with a large round dot; U-N-T are welded into one shape; the T's bar fuses to the N. It ends with a large circular full stop, the "punt" of the name, about the size of a counter. Trademark symbol at top right as a deadpan detail.
- **Variants:** one-line wordmark; two-line stacked version (PA i / PUNT.) on labels (observed); the full stop can stand alone as a dot motif (inferred).

## Wordmark / lettering

Custom; caps with a lowercase i; negative tracking to the point of fusion; the heaviness reads as a raw lump of dough (inferred).

## Type system

- **Teodor** (Displaay): a sharp, classic serif with a raw edge, which Pérez likened to slicing through bread; used large for manifesto quotes in sentence case with typographic quotation marks.
- **Neue Haas Grotesk Regular/Display** (Commercial Type): neutral counterpart; opening hours, phone numbers, addresses, price lists, coordinates; often caps, tight, sometimes huge (hours set as headlines).
- **Type moves:** information treated as display (days, hours and phone number set at poster scale); coordinates (41.5333 - 2.0833) and "Des de ©2020" used as graphic tags; contact rows framed in thin pill-shaped outlines; bottom-of-poster manifesto in small caps.

## Colour system

No hexes published. Observed: one solid ground per piece, chosen from a small set: pale acid yellow, sage/mint green, bread-crust terracotta orange, off-white/paper, black; all type and forms in black; where text crosses a black form it switches to the ground colour (knock-out). Rule: one ground colour plus black per application.

## Device(s) / graphic language

- **The three-layer system (published rule):** layer 1, a solid colour background; layer 2, strong typographic compositions; layer 3, disruptive organic forms that break into that order. Any application is built by stacking these three; elements can be rearranged or duplicated while staying consistent.
- **Organic forms:** black, ragged shapes made by analogue means (tearing, printing, trial and error) and then digitised, echoing crumb holes and crust cracks; unpredictable in shape, consistent in logic; they cover or cut through type, bleed off edges and sit behind quotes.
- **Torn wheat ear:** one recurring figurative form, a jagged stalk silhouette.

## Imagery

Bread photographed plainly: sliced loaves on white with a knife standing in them, rolls on bakery racks, the baker at work in a black branded T-shirt. Direct, flash-like, no styling props (observed). Never: soft-focus flour dust, rustic wooden boards, pastoral wheat fields (inferred).

## Voice

Catalan, first person plural, combative and principled: "we are artisans", "we want a change", the right to decent bread, a starting point that educates towards quality food. Short declaratives. The name itself is the tone: bread, full stop.

## Applications

Posters, A-board street sign, shop signage, menu and price lists, clipboard order menu, bread bags and labels, T-shirts and caps, printed sheets, social posts.
**What varies:** ground colour, position and shape of organic forms, which information is set large. **What stays fixed:** wordmark with full stop, black ink, Teodor + Neue Haas Grotesk, the three-layer logic.

## Guidelines structure

Not published; the three-layer rule is the stated operating guideline for the client.

## What makes it ownable

The fused, dotted wordmark ending in a big full stop; black torn crumb-shapes breaking layouts; manifesto posters in Teodor on acid colours.

## What a generic version would have been

A hand-script "Pa i Punt" in brown with a wheat sprig, kraft bags with a stamp, cream and ochre palette, "artesà des de 2020" in a vintage badge.

## Principles it demonstrates

1. Look at how the product is physically made and turn a by-product of the process into the graphic device.
2. Write the system as a small number of layers (ground, type, disruption) so a non-designer can produce new pieces that stay coherent.
3. Let a stance, not a mood, set the register: a bakery with a cause can borrow the language of protest posters.
4. Build the name's meaning into the mark (the full stop is literally the "punt").
5. Pair one expressive serif with one neutral grotesque, and make practical information large enough to be the headline.
