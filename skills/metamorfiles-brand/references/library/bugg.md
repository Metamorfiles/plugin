# Bugg

- **Name / business / place / year / studio:** Bugg, premium garden tools, wear and accessories sold online (bugg.co.nz), a new sibling brand of the New Zealand garden retailer Gubba. Auckland, New Zealand. 2025. Seachange (Auckland/Sydney; creative directors Amanda Gaskin and Tim Donaldson; team George Boyd, Nicole McClure, Lily Savage): naming, strategy, identity, packaging, art direction. Brand New (Noted, Sep 2025), BP&O (Nov 2025), NZ Best Design Awards 2025 (Small Brand Identity).

## Brief & business truth
Gubba already sold garden tools. Bugg is a premium line for dedicated, design-literate gardeners: tools (secateurs, hori hori, trowels), grow, tidy, wear and gifts. The category is either wholesome-rustic (kraft, botanical line art, sage green) or hardware-store functional. Bugg needed to feel premium and giftable while remaining warm.

## Strategy / insight
Serious gardeners are obsessives, a subculture with its own taste and codes. Treat gardening as an identity people wear, not a quaint hobby, and speak to the obsession with affection and wit. "Premium" and "cute" can live together if the craft is high.

## The idea (one sentence) and its source
The bugs you'd normally keep out of the garden become the brand's cast, starting with the name and the wordmark's two antenna-g's, so the brand lives down among the critters with its gardeners. **Source:** the name (Bugg: insects plus the "bug" of an obsession).

## Register
Character world with editorial fashion polish: cute but refined.

## Category codes it used or broke
- Broke: sage-and-kraft rusticity, botanical line drawings, smiling-grandparent lifestyle photography, green-on-green palettes, earnest sustainability copy.
- Used and re-cut: insects (usually pests) recast as mascots and jewellery; earthy materials (uncoated board, soil photographs) kept as a ground for acid colour.

## Mark
- Wordmark: lowercase "bugg" in a very heavy, wide, low-contrast custom face with slightly flared, wedge-ended strokes (the b's lower spur is a small wedge).
- Construction of the g's: two identical double-storey g's. Each upper bowl is a wide ellipse with a tiny, narrow vertical-slot counter that reads like an eye; the lower loop is a broad flattened ellipse ending in an angled cut. The ear of each g is replaced by an upright stem rising from the bowl and ending in a teardrop blob with a diagonal notch, like an antenna tip or an unfurling sprout. Two g's side by side make a pair of antennae and a pair of faces.
- The ® sits at the baseline after the last g.
- Variants: wordmark alone; wordmark small with "Premium products for people who live in the garden" as a two-line sign-off; wordmark repeated (packing tape), including upside-down and mirrored instances.

## Wordmark / lettering
Custom heavy lowercase, tight letter spacing, the g's are the only modified letters. The contrast between the chunky wordmark and the narrow text face is part of the look.

## Type system
- Perfektta (Displaay, Czech): primary typeface, a sans with narrow proportions and visible stem contrast; used in bold for headlines and product names, regular for small copy. Headlines set tight, sentence case, flush left, in large blocks (the "dirt cult" manifesto fills a whole panel).
- Small print jokes set in tiny type under product names ("* Caterpillar not included").

## Colour system
Earthy, mineral base plus acid accents (BP&O). Values sampled from published images (inferred):
- Deep forest/moss green, about #1D2F21: primary dark, backgrounds and wordmark.
- Acid lime, about #EBF987: signature accent (wordmark ground, headlines on dark, packing tape).
- Compost/clay brown, about #7D4401: tags, labels.
- Photo backgrounds: soft sky gradient from pale blue (about #9BB6C9) to blush (about #D4C1BB).
- Products supply their own saturated colours (red bag, green can) against the soft gradient.
- Rule (inferred): lime is paired with forest green or brown, never with white.

## Device(s) / graphic language
1. **Critters as co-stars:** a real insect (caterpillar, ladybirds, beetle, snail, butterflies) sits on each product in the photograph, with a footnote disclaiming it ("* Snail not included").
2. **Product cards:** rounded-corner photo cards on a forest green field, product name in lime Perfektta at top left, footnote beneath.
3. **Tag shape:** a swing tag with a pointed (pennant) base and rounded top corners, brown with lime type; some tags are die-cut to look nibbled.
4. **Micro details:** micro-embossed insect forms on labels, packing tape with lines of marching ants and repeated wordmarks, quiet jokes in instruction cards.
5. **Illustrated critters:** used sparingly on the homepage, packaging edges and guides; a ladybird character appears on the site.

## Imagery
Two modes: fashion-editorial portraits of young gardeners in styled garden workwear, with insects as jewellery or companions (butterflies around a model holding a planter bag); and still lifes of tools against a pastel sky gradient, each "visited" by one bug. Sharp, clean studio light. Soil appears only as texture (tag photographed on dark compost). Never: muddy hands, old-fashioned potting sheds, stock garden scenes.

## Voice
Witty, self-aware, affectionate about obsession. Samples: "For people who own more secateurs than shoes" (BP&O); a tag that calls finding soil acidity a conversation starter; a manifesto that starts "Welcome to the dirt cult." and describes worship of sun and rain and whispering to seedlings, ending that this is an obsession, not a hobby. Rules (inferred): talk to insiders, exaggerate devotion, keep product copy plain and factual underneath the jokes.

## Applications
Posters and manifesto panels, product cards and e-commerce imagery, swing tags, labels with embossing, packing tape, shipping boxes, instruction cards, website (homepage illustration, ladybird), social.
- **Fixed:** wordmark, forest/lime/brown palette, Perfektta, one-bug-per-product photographic rule.
- **Varies:** which critter, the product, background (gradient sky vs. soil vs. solid green), joke line.

## Guidelines structure
None published.

## What makes it ownable
The antenna g's; lime on forest green; the one-bug photo convention with its "not included" footnote; the nibbled tag and marching-ant tape.

## What a generic version would have been
A serif or rounded wordmark with a leaf or trowel icon, sage and cream on kraft paper, watercolour botanicals, and lifestyle photos of gardeners in a sunny allotment.

## Principles it demonstrates
- Mine the name for a single letterform modification that carries the character, and keep the rest of the wordmark plain.
- Invite the category's villain (pests) into the brand as its charm.
- Put one repeatable, witty rule into the photography (one bug, one footnote) so every image is on-brand without a logo.
- Pair an earthy base palette with one acid accent to lift a dowdy category into fashion.
- Hide small rewards (nibbled tags, ant tape, jokes in instructions) for customers who look closely.
