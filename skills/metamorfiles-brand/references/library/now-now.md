# Now Now

- **Name:** Now Now ("lodging club")
- **Business:** Pod hotel of small, high-design, single-occupancy sleeper cabins with shared bathrooms, for solo travellers; seventh hotel from Dovetail + Co.; restored 1917 building on the Bowery
- **Place:** NoHo, Manhattan, New York, USA
- **Year:** 2025
- **Studio:** Saint-Urbain, New York (founder/CD Alex Ostroff; AD/design Guillaume Lavallée, Genaro Garmilla; type design Guillaume Lavallée; 3D Oli Sabin, Daniel Schriër; photography Hunter Kelley)

## Brief & business truth

Rooms are tiny; the audience is young solo travellers who want to be out in the city and off their phones, not in a destination hotel. The brief: an identity that feels distinctly "now" for today's solo traveller but can last as a hospitality brand, and that turns the small room from a weakness into a selling point. Touchpoints: collateral, outdoor, motion, digital templates, merch, signage, social.

## Strategy / insight

Starting thought (studio): the hotel is not a destination but part of the journey. The hotel is a liminal space, a point of transition, restoration and launch. So the identity is about thresholds (in and out, room and city) and about presence: living in the now, a quiet community, a pause. Tagline: "Be Where You Belong". The brand should never shout.

## The idea

**One sentence:** Every letter of the name is a tiny room with a keyhole cut into it, so the logo itself says "small space, gateway to everything outside".
**Source:** moment (the present tense of the name, "now") combined with a reversal of hotel codes (a hotel that wants you to leave your room).

## Register

Retro-futurist modular / soft graphic; "Kubrick-esque" warmth; calm rather than loud, but with a strong geometric mark.

## Category codes it used or broke

- Broke: luxury hotel codes (serif monogram, crest, gold, black-and-white restraint, room-as-sanctuary imagery). The room is sold as a launchpad, and the ads admit it is small.
- Broke: single brand colour. A shifting gradient palette acts like a mood ring.
- Used: classic hotel objects (key cards, door hangers, bathrobes, slippers, mints) as the main canvas, but each carries a joke or a nudge outward.

## Mark

- **Type:** modular wordmark made of tiles.
- **Construction:** each letter is a solid rectangular tile, slightly taller than wide, separated from its neighbours by a narrow gap (about 1/20 of tile width, estimated). Letters are cut out as negative space from circles and straight slots: O = one circle punched in the centre; N = a single slanted slit from top to bottom with two circular "ball" ends on either side, reading as a keyhole; W = two vertical slots that end in circles at the bottom, like two inverted keyholes side by side. Only circles, straight cuts and rectangles. The keyhole (circle + slot) is the unit; the tiles stand for the compact rooms.
- **Arrangements (three, published):** a horizontal row of six tiles (N-O-W-N-O-W); a 3x2 stacked block (NOW over NOW); a plus-sign cross of five tiles that shares the O, reading NOW across and N-O-W down.
- **Small size:** the stacked block and the cross work as icons on slippers, bathrobes, key cards and mints (observed).

## Wordmark / lettering

Custom (Guillaume Lavallée). Geometric, stencil-like, no curves except the circles. Always one colour, reversed out of a tile.

## Type system

- **Ginestra** (&Rosàs / Íñigo Jerez, via extratype): primary, in Black weight for headlines; a condensed humanist sans rooted in 19th-century posters, created in memory of Nacho Ginestra; kerning set at -10 in the specimen; used large, tight, often two-colour stacked headlines.
- **Saans** (Displaay), Medium and Regular: text; neutral, quiet guidance, kerning -10.
- Rule: the wordmark is the graphic hero; typefaces stay readable and human.

## Colour system

No hexes published. Observed palette of warm neutrals and a few bright accents, aligned with the interiors: cream/peach, dark brown, plum/burgundy, candy pink/lilac, deep teal green, mint/lime green. Pairs used: brown on cream, pink on plum, lime on teal. Gradients blend plum, bottle green and lime (and dark navy) as soft glowing fields behind the mark or on key cards and mint tins, intended to shift with mood or time of day.

## Device(s) / graphic language

- **Tiles and keyholes:** the mark's tile grid is reused as layout modules (logo tiles stacked at the edges of posters as frames or bookends).
- **Mood-ring gradients:** blurred multi-colour fields used as grounds, never on type.
- **Photo + colour-block split:** billboards and posters split into a flat colour panel with a Ginestra headline and a candid photo panel.
- **3D renders:** soft 3D objects for motion and digital (credited to Oli Sabin, Daniel Schriër).

## Imagery

Photography by Hunter Kelley: young solo travellers out in the city, jumping, lounging on rooftops, reading, looking up; warm late-afternoon light, film-like colour; in-room shots show the narrow corridor of doors, robe on a hook. Never: wide empty luxury suites or bed-with-towel-swan shots (inferred).

## Voice

Warm, witty, present-tense, honest about size, pushing outward. Sample lines: "Small Rooms, Big Adventures", "A hotel for curious minds and creative spirits", "Your Room is Ready!", "Be Where You Belong", a "Not Right Now" door hanger, "Mints Now", a "Now or Never" sweatshirt. Plays on "now" repeatedly.

## Applications

Billboards and wild posters, key cards, door hangers, bathrobes, slides/slippers, mint tins, hoodies and sweatshirts, signage, website, digital templates, motion, social.
**What varies:** arrangement of the wordmark (row, block, cross), colour pair, gradient, headline wordplay. **What stays fixed:** the keyhole tile letters, Ginestra + Saans, the warm six-colour family, candid city photography.

## Guidelines structure

Not published. Observed deliverables: logo arrangements, typography specimen (Ginestra Black / Saans with kerning values), colour and gradients, layout splits, collateral, merch, OOH, web.

## What makes it ownable

The keyhole tile letters and their three arrangements; wordplay on "now"; plum/pink and teal/lime pairings with gradient grounds.

## What a generic version would have been

A thin serif wordmark in black or gold, a minimal "NN" monogram, beige-and-white palette, hero shots of the cabin interior, copy about "curated micro-luxury".

## Principles it demonstrates

1. Turn the product's apparent weakness (tiny rooms) into the mark's construction and the headline.
2. Build a wordmark from a small kit of primitives (rectangle, circle, slot) so it can re-arrange into rows, blocks and crosses without redrawing.
3. Make the name's key word a copy engine: one repeated word can generate a whole voice.
4. Use colour pairs and a gradient ground as a flexible mood system instead of one fixed brand colour.
5. Treat the guest's small objects (key card, door hanger, slippers, mints) as the primary media, each carrying one idea.
