# Mockup taste

What strong packaging photography looks like, from a study of work design publications feature. It
describes; it never decides: the brand's direction and what it makes decide the shots.

## Strong

- **One idea per photograph,** readable at a glance: a pattern, a stack, a pour, a detail.
- **An idea from the brand's world:** a moment, a place or a ritual of this business, which no other
  brand's shot could be.
- **Materials you can feel:** uncoated paper, tube board, kraft, matte ceramic, brushed metal.
- **Packaging designed in full:** a big confident wordmark, one bold graphic idea, a clear hierarchy.

## Weak, and why it happens

- **A logo on a flat field of colour:** the prompt gave the face no idea, only the logo. The
  packaging wasn't designed, only labelled.
- **Parts stacked, not designed:** a strip with the logo, a window with a photograph, the name under
  it. The prompt measured the face out part by part, or gave it no idea to hold it together. The
  brand's photographs go on a pack only when the idea is a photograph.
- **A redrawn or misspelled logo:** the prompt didn't hold the model to image 1. Say what image 1 is
  and that its letters stay exactly as they are.
- **A print laid over the photograph:** the prompt didn't say how the mark is made, on what material,
  following which surface, in which light. Say all four.
- **An object missing its parts or out of shape:** the prompt didn't describe the whole object as
  it's really made. Say its parts.
- **Garbled small print:** the prompt asked for text the brief doesn't give. Ask for none.
- **Objects that couldn't exist:** a lid fused to its jar, an opening on the wrong face, contents
  outside their pack. The physics weren't said; say them.
- **A glossy, rendered look:** smooth surfaces, perfect reflections, a retouched-ad feel. Describe a
  real moment and its real materials, with signs of use, and no studio wording.
