# Mockup taste

What strong packaging photography looks like, from a study of work design publications feature. It
describes; it never decides: the brand's direction and what it makes decide the shots.

## Strong

- **One idea per photograph,** readable at a glance: a pattern, a stack, a pour, a detail.
- **An idea from the brand's world:** a moment, a place or a ritual of this business, which no other
  brand's shot could be.
- **Materials you can feel:** uncoated paper, tube board, kraft, matte ceramic, brushed metal.
- **Packaging designed in full:** a big confident wordmark, one bold graphic idea, a clear hierarchy.

## Weak, and why it happens

- **A twist with no reason:** one unit of a pattern turned or lifted because a pattern "should" break.
  It reads as an error; a break is another object, there for a reason.
- **A logo on a flat field of colour:** the packaging wasn't designed, only labelled.
- **Parts stacked, not designed:** a strip with the logo, a window with a photograph, the name under
  it. No idea holds the face together. The brand's photographs go on a pack only when the idea is a
  photograph.
- **A redrawn or misspelled logo:** the prompt didn't hold the model to image 1. Edit it back.
- **Garbled small print:** the prompt asked for text the brief doesn't give. Ask for none.
- **Objects that couldn't exist:** a lid fused to its tin, an opening on the wrong face, contents
  outside their pack. The physics weren't said; say them and edit.
- **A glossy, rendered look:** smooth plastic surfaces and perfect reflections. Name a textured,
  matte finish.
