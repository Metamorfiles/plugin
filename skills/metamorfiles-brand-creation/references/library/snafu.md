## Brief & business truth
A small, host-led podcast brand (one flagship show, with ambitions to be a media label) in the crowded true-story/history podcast category. Its cover art may be the only visual listeners ever see, at thumbnail size in a podcast app, so it must: stand out from look-alike covers, make clear that Ed Helms hosts, and convey what the storytelling feels like. It also needed a system for episodes, social, web, live shows and merch. The client framed the show as a rallying cry for the underdog and a celebration of glorious mess-ups that pokes at authority.

## Strategy / insight
Investigative and history podcasts play it safe with clean, tidy, minimal identities. A show about screw-ups should look like it was pulled from a vault of government secrets, with controlled messiness: serious, but not that serious. Fresneda's formulation of the balance was irreverence versus professionalism.

## The idea (one sentence)
Declassified pulp: the brand looks like a 1970s top-secret file crossed with a Cold War spy-movie poster, with a monumental logo casting a long shadow, and Ed Helms as a news anchor sitting calmly in the middle of the mess.
**Source:** the name (SNAFU, military slang for "situation normal, all f***ed up") and the moment/subject (Cold War secrecy, the 1971 FBI files), expressed by reversing the category's clean-minimal code.

## Register
Retro pulp / editorial poster: 1950s–70s propaganda, spy-poster and paperback-cover codes, with collage and redaction.

## Category codes it used or broke
- Broke: the minimal, tasteful history/investigative podcast cover (serif title, muted photo, lots of space).
- Used, then twisted: celebrity-host covers (a host photo is standard). Here Ed is a cut-out anchor at a news desk with a stack of papers, a deadpan joke on authority.
- Borrowed: Cold War propaganda books, 1950s–60s spy-movie posters, vintage adventure/educational book covers, pulp novels, newspaper headlines, FBI documents with black redaction bars and red stamps.

## Mark
- **Type:** wordmark with a built-in extruded shadow.
- **Construction:** "SNAFU" in capitals from a 1970s geometric display face: round-cornered, monoline, heavy strokes; the A is a pointed, triangular form whose left leg is a thin diagonal and whose right side is a heavy vertical, with a small triangular counter; the N and U have rounded outer corners; S and F are wide (letter details read from the images, inferred). The letters are off-white, outlined by a thick black keyline that follows the outer contour. Behind them a long, solid black shadow is extruded down and to the right at about 45°, running out of frame on large formats, so the word reads like a monument casting a long shadow at sunset (the studio's own image), or like a decoder overlay.
- **Variants:** off-white letters + black shadow on red (primary); red letters + dark shadow on the cover; small version on stickers and socks with a short shadow; the shadow length changes by format (long and frame-breaking on screens and OOH, short on merch and business cards).

## Wordmark / lettering
Capitals only, tight spacing, heavy weight, rounded geometric letterforms with 70s quirks. Tracking tight so letters nearly touch; the shadow unifies them into one block.

## Type system
- **Headline display face** (Creative Boom names Marvin Visions; the case credits Displaay Type Foundry, likely for the companion text face, inferred): all caps, dramatic, slightly angular, "authoritative and a little quirky"; used for the logo, headlines ("G-MEN ARE COMING TO TOWN", "REDACTED", "FINAL EPISODE", "DISCOVER THE GREATEST SCREW-UPS IN HISTORY") and sparingly for short copy.
- **Supporting text:** a clean grotesk for body, dates and UI (inferred as Displaay's Saans from the case credit).
- Type-as-image: headline stacked in 3–4 tight lines; repeated lines shrinking in size ("SCREWED UP!" x6), like a stutter or an echo.

## Colour system (published)
- **Top Secret Red** #E64244
- **Off White** #F5F5DF (the colour of aged government paper)
- **Jet Black** #231F20
Near-black and cream carry the 1970s-file mood; red gives contemporary boldness and marks classified/urgent. Typical splits (inferred): red field with off-white type and black shadow; black field with red type; cream paper collage with red and black accents. Ed's blue suit is the only non-palette colour and makes him pop.

## Device(s) / graphic language
- **Long shadow:** the logo's extrusion is reused on headlines and frames.
- **Redaction:** black bars over typewritten text, torn/burnt-edge black holes cut into collages (the black hole behind Ed on the cover).
- **Document collage:** typewritten FBI memos, red rubber stamps (FBI seal), handwritten notes, file folders, as backgrounds.
- **Transparent red overlays** (like a decoder filter) over black-and-white archive photos, plus rough red brush strokes.
- **Rounded TV frame:** portraits in a thick-bordered rounded rectangle like an old television.
- **Film grain / dust and vignetted rounded corners** on motion frames.

## Imagery
Black-and-white archive photography (protests, officials such as J. Edgar Hoover), halftoned and overlaid with red; Ed Helms as a cut-out, saturated colour, at a news desk or as a big-head caricature in a suit, deadpan expression; collaged paper artefacts. Never glossy modern stock or clean studio shots.

## Voice
Irreverent, punchy, tabloid headline energy with real journalistic weight: "Greatest screw-ups", "Screwed up!", "G-men are coming to town", "Discover the greatest screw-ups in history", "Embrace the screw". Jokes live in headlines; the storytelling stays serious.

## Applications
Podcast cover art (season 2), episode tiles, in-app player screens, social posts and stories, website, live recording/auditorium screen, billboards/large screens, business cards, tote bags, coach jacket, socks, a "final episode" announcement.
What varies: episode subject imagery, headline, which colour leads, shadow length. What stays fixed: SNAFU wordmark with keyline and shadow, three colours, redaction/document texture, the display face in caps.

## Guidelines structure
Not public. The case shows: logo, typeface specimen (headline alphabet and numerals), colour (three named swatches with hex), cover art, episode/social templates, web, merch.

## What makes it ownable
The long-shadow SNAFU block; Top Secret Red + file cream + jet black; redaction bars and FBI-paper collage; Ed as deadpan anchor in the middle of chaos.

## What a generic version would have been
A dark moody photo of the host with a thin serif title, a sepia map or old photo background, and a tasteful muted palette like every other history podcast.

## Principles it demonstrates
- Design the smallest, most-seen touchpoint first (a podcast cover at thumbnail size) and let the system grow from it.
- Take the brand's name literally and give it a period and a place (Cold War files) so the look has a source, not just a mood.
- Break a category's safe code when the content is about breaking rules; keep one element (the host at a desk) calm as an anchor.
- Hold a maximal collage together with a strict three-colour palette and one display face.
- Make a single device (the long shadow) do double duty as logo feature and system motif, flexing its length by format.
