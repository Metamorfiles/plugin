# Caffè Nazionale

- **Name:** Caffè Nazionale
- **Business:** Historic café-bar and bistro (espresso at the bar, food, evening events), open 7am to midnight, seven days
- **Place:** Piazza Libertà, Arzignano (Vicenza), Veneto, Italy
- **Year:** 2024-2025 (griffon sketches dated May 2024; reopened and published early 2025; BP&O review March 2026)
- **Studio:** Studio Mut, Bolzano (Martin Kerschbaumer). Art and lettering: Stefan Marx (Berlin), typeface produced with Dinamo. Architecture and project lead: AMAA (co-founder Marcello Galiotto, an Arzignano native, brought Studio Mut in).

## Brief & business truth

The Caffè had been the social centre of Arzignano since the 1950s (espresso at the counter, cards, billiards) and closed around 2018. In 2022 the town launched a revival; entrepreneur Marco Mettifogo, pastry chef Andrea Poli and AMAA reopened it in the porticoed town hall. Coffee at the bar costs one euro. The brief was open: bring the bar back without making it feel like a new "brand" or an Instagram destination. Audience: everyone in town, all day, from morning espresso to late events.

## Strategy / insight

Kerschbaumer framed the project as restitution: giving the café back to the people it belongs to. So the identity should look accumulated from the place rather than designed for it, and should use what is already in collective memory (the griffon on the piazza column, local dialect, street signage) rather than invent new symbols.

## The idea

**One sentence:** The identity is made from the town's own visual and spoken vernacular (street shop lettering, Veneto dialect and the piazza's griffon) so the café looks like it has always been there.
**Source:** place (the piazza, its streets, its speech and its heraldic creature).

## Register

Vernacular / street-sign directness with a character world; "painted shop sign" confidence, classy rather than folksy.

## Category codes it used or broke

- Used: the classic Italian bar kit (red-and-white, porcelain espresso cups with the name, sugar sachets, paper napkins, receipts, printed paper bags), taken completely straight.
- Broke: Italian heritage nostalgia (script logos, gilded Liberty ornament, tricolore) and specialty-coffee minimalism. The heritage is carried by vernacular lettering and dialect, not by retro styling.
- Broke: heraldry. The town's griffon is taken off its pedestal and turned into a regular at the bar.

## Mark

- **Type:** wordmark "CAFFÈ NAZIONALE" plus a mascot (the griffon, "grifone").
- **Wordmark construction:** all caps, a condensed, slightly irregular hand-drawn sans in the manner of painted shop fascias; near-uniform stroke, flat terminals, slightly wobbly verticals, grave accent on the È. Set on one line; on napkins, bags and the espresso cup it bends onto an arc like lettering on a round shop sign (observed). "ARZIGNANO" can sit beneath in the handwriting face, widely letterspaced.
- **Mascot construction:** mythological creature (lion body, eagle wings, snake tail) that stands on a column in the piazza and was once crudely painted, with three stars, on the old café door. Redrawn by Stefan Marx in thin, wobbly black monoline with no fills, open contours, little stars and sparkles around it. Poses change: carrying an espresso, reading a newspaper, lounging, sleeping, arms raised.
- **Variants:** straight wordmark; arched wordmark; wordmark + place name; griffon scenes of any size; small pictograms (cup, stars) (observed).

## Wordmark / lettering

The whole typographic voice derives from lettering on panini vans, parking signs, salumerie, barbershops, laundries, pizzerias and car workshops around the piazza. Marx distilled these into a bespoke handwriting font produced with Dinamo: loose, sprawling, faux-naïf capitals, used for headlines, posters and short phrases.

## Type system

- **Custom handwriting face (Marx/Dinamo):** personality; headlines, event posters, dialect phrases, place names. Caps.
- **Helvetica Neue Medium:** the quiet counterweight; addresses, menus, opening hours, admin text; set in caps, flush left, tight stacks (observed on bags and coasters).
- **Type-as-image moves:** posters where every line is handwritten at a different scale with doodles (clock, sparkles, thumbs up) dropped between words; a "Burger Night" poster with a red blob-lettered title melting into a griffon drawing; the wordmark repeated as a wall of stacked lines on a window.

## Colour system

Black, white and red only, across everything (published by BP&O/Abduzeedo; no hex given). Red = the name (wordmark, cups, paper bags, notebook); black = drawings and information; white = paper, porcelain, ground. Exceptions are material (a kraft sugar sachet, a bright orange cap, a red neon "CIN CIN" in the bar) (observed). Rule (inferred): colour never becomes a gradient or tint; each item is one ink, like a cheap commercial print job.

## Device(s) / graphic language

- **The griffon as a regular:** a recurring character acting out the café's daily rituals; placed large on bags and cups, small on saucers, coasters and sugar sachets; always line-only, always in one colour.
- **Dialect lines:** phrases from everyday Veneto speech act as graphic elements, e.g. the motto "Se magna, se beve, se sta ben" (you eat, you drink, you're doing well), "No stà savatare" on the take-away bag, "CIN CIN" on coasters and in neon.
- **Stacked info blocks:** small caps Helvetica lines (bar / bistro / in piazza libertà ad Arzignano) beside a big drawing, like a shop label.

## Imagery

Mostly drawing, not photography. Photography of the place is warm, documentary (bartenders in overalls and griffon T-shirts, pouring milk into the named cup, evening crowds under red light). Never: styled latte-art hero shots or nostalgic sepia archive imagery (inferred).

## Voice

Dialect, short, generous, unpretentious; the one-euro espresso is the tone. Sample lines: the motto above; "Cin cin"; event posters listing dishes and bands in plain words. Italian and Veneto, never English lifestyle copy (inferred).

## Applications

Porcelain espresso cups and saucers (wordmark on cup, griffon on saucer), paper cups, paper carrier bags, bakery bags, napkins, coasters, sugar sachets, receipts, pastry boxes, menu clipboards, notebooks, uniforms and caps, neon sign, window graphics, website, social posts, event posters (jazz nights, Oktoberfest, burger nights).
**What varies:** griffon pose and scale, which dialect line, arched or straight wordmark, poster composition. **What stays fixed:** the three colours, the handwriting face plus Helvetica, the line-only drawing style.

## Guidelines structure

None published.

## What makes it ownable

The griffon doing ordinary bar things; the sign-painter caps in red; the Veneto dialect lines; strict black-white-red on humble paper goods.

## What a generic version would have been

A gold-foil Liberty-style script "Caffè Nazionale 1950", a coffee-bean or cup icon, tricolore accents, espresso-brown palette, English taglines about "authentic Italian coffee culture".

## Principles it demonstrates

1. Source the lettering from the street the business stands on, so the identity reads as part of the place rather than as a launch.
2. Take a symbol people already know and change its behaviour (heraldic figure becomes a regular), rather than inventing a new mascot.
3. Pair one characterful face with one neutral face and give each a fixed job.
4. A three-colour limit lets loose drawing and lettering stay coherent across dozens of cheap objects.
5. Write in the customers' own dialect; words can carry place better than images.
