# Headspace (2023–2024 rebrand)

## Name / business / place / year / studio
- **Name:** Headspace
- **Business:** consumer and employer mental-health platform. Started as a meditation app in 2010. In 2021 it merged with the clinical provider Ginger, and it now offers mindfulness, coaching, therapy and psychiatry in one app, with the clinical part called Headspace Care.
- **Place:** Santa Monica / San Francisco, USA. Used in 190 countries.
- **Year:** the brand guidelines were built through 2023: logo sizzle dated May 2023, guidelines "last updated June 2023", then November 2023, then September 2024. The brand unification (Ginger renamed Headspace Care) was announced 31 Oct 2023. The press launch of the new look came in late April 2024 (It's Nice That, 25 Apr 2024; Brand New, 20 May 2024).
- **Studio:** Headspace's in-house design team with ITAL/C (Italic Studio, California/Virginia), over about six months. The custom typeface **Headspace Aperçu** is by Colophon Foundry. The guidelines site was built by Order on the Standards platform.

## Brief & business truth
Headspace was the best-known meditation app. Its flat orange-dot, smiling-character style had become an icon of "mindfulness." After the Ginger merger it had to sell and deliver clinical care (therapy, psychiatry, EAP) to employers and health plans. It also had to keep reaching consumers now that meditation had gone mainstream. The old brand was cheerful but narrow. It read as calm and joy, which is awkward next to depression or a psychiatric appointment. The old palette also had accessibility problems. The identity needed to:
- stretch from playful to clinical without splitting into two brands
- carry real people (coaches, therapists) as well as cartoons
- stay instantly recognisable to 100M+ downloaders
- meet WCAG AA

This was an evolution. The dot, the orange, the smile and the illustrated world were kept and pushed further.

## Strategy / insight
Mental health is not a single mood. The brand had only ever shown the happy end of the spectrum (a meditating smile). To help people who are stressed, sad or stuck, it had to acknowledge those feelings without dwelling on misery. The brand's own framing is that imperfection is part of being human. The logo circle was redrawn as an *imperfect circle*, and the guidelines sum up the stance as "Not perfect, but whole." Colour was used to stand apart from a mental-health category full of clinical blues and greys. Personality became the tool for destigmatising care: approachable enough that talking about your mind feels normal.

## The idea (one sentence) and its source
**Idea:** A family of simple flat shapes with faces, led by the calm orange dot, that together show the whole range of human feelings, so every mood has a friendly face in the brand.
**Source:** benefit / emotion. The feelings of mental health are made into characters, with the founding mark as the lead character.

## Register
Character world: flat, bright, cartoon shapes with faces. It is balanced by a clean humanist-grotesque type system and candid photography, so the system can turn down to "clinical" or up to "playful."

## Category codes it used or broke
- **Broke:** healthcare's safe blues, greys, teal, stethoscope and leaf imagery. Headspace uses saturated orange, yellow, pink and green, with characters instead of clinicians.
- **Broke:** wellness clichés (lotus, ohm, zen stones, sunsets, mountaintop meditators). The guidelines ban them in words (Zen, Chi, Nirvana, Chakra, Guru, Transcend, Retreat, Higher self) and in pictures (everyday meditation on a commute rather than a mountaintop retreat).
- **Broke:** literal illustration of distress. There are no crying people or grey rain clouds. Hard feelings are shown as metaphor and through the essence of the emotion.
- **Used:** the meditation code of orange (the dot's colour references the robes and traditions of meditation practice, per It's Nice That and the guidelines). The closed-eye serene face is used too, but as a friendly cartoon rather than a religious image.
- **Used:** real clinicians and coaches in photography, as healthcare trust requires, always given a brand-colour or illustrative layer.

## Mark
- **Type:** symbol plus wordmark. The symbol is the "Imperfect Circle" brand mark, a solid orange dot. In illustration the same dot becomes a character with a face.
- **Construction:** a filled circle drawn slightly off-true by hand. In the official SVG it is about 366.5 × 362.8 units, so a hair wider than tall. The outline is one closed freehand curve with small, uneven bulges and flats (the upper left is fuller, the lower right slightly flattened). It reads as a circle at a glance and as hand-made up close. There is no outline, gradient or inner detail, and no face in the logo. The face lives only in the character version.
- **Rule split:** the *logo* uses the imperfect circle. When the dot is *illustrated as a character*, it must be a perfect circle. The guidelines state both directions as explicit do/don't rules.
- **Colour:** dot Orange 200 #FF7300. Wordmark Warm Grey 700 #2D2C2B (from the SVG fill).
- **Lockup:** horizontal, with the dot on the left and the wordmark on the right. The dot is about 2.2× the height of the lowercase ascenders and centred on the word. The gap is about a quarter of the dot's diameter (measured from SVG, inferred). The lowercase "h" is the spacing unit for the mark's size and placement. Clear space is one imperfect-circle diameter on all four sides.
- **Variants:** primary (orange dot + warm-grey word) on light, warm grey or yellow. Inverted (orange dot + white word) on dark warm grey and Sleep indigo. All-white (white dot + white word) on photography and brand colours, which is how it appears on the blue, green and orange ads. Black is a last resort. There is a registered ® version for first touchpoints (site, app splash) and partnerships, and a "small use" logo for tight banners. Brand-mark-only is allowed just for app icon, social avatar and favicon.
- **Minimum size:** 120 px wide digital, 1.25 in wide print.
- **Partnerships:** Headspace logo, then a thin vertical divider, then the partner logo, kept within the height of the circle (the red safe zone allows taller marks). The order is never reversed, the sizes never unequal, and the divider stroke never changed.
- **Don'ts:** stretch, effects, outline, pattern fills, rotation, unapproved colours, primary or inverted logo over photos.

## Wordmark / lettering
- Lowercase "headspace", one word, set in the Aperçu family at a regular/book weight (not bold).
- The a is double-storey and the e has a flat horizontal bar. Round bowls, straight vertical stems and slightly open terminals give it the calm, geometric-humanist feel of Aperçu (Johnston/Gill/Neuzeit lineage).
- Tracking is neutral to slightly open. There are no special ligatures. The personality comes from the dot, not from modified letters (inferred from the SVG).

## Type system
- **Headspace Aperçu** (custom cut of Colophon's Aperçu): Light, Regular, Medium, Bold, ExtraBold, Black, each with italics. **Aperçu Mono** (separate licence) for small technical text.
- **Custom features:** curves in some letterforms nod to the smile arc. There are playful "scripted" alternates with calmer standard versions, and expressive punctuation. Small-currency and percent glyphs are set for UI and stats. The hero smile is built in as a glyph, typed by the shortcut `_HERO_`, so the face can sit inline after a line of text.
- **Jobs and specs (from the guidelines):**
  - Black: illustrated type and bold declarative moments.
  - Bold: headlines, e.g. 120 px, tracking -30, leading 100%.
  - Medium: subheads and pull quotes, 30 px, tracking -20, leading 140%.
  - Regular: body, 18 px, tracking -10, leading 140%.
  - Mono: annotations, dates, page numbers, 12 px, tracking -10.
- **Case:** sentence case throughout. Campaign type also goes all lowercase (bus: "how are you today?").
- **Type-as-image moves:**
  - Headlines curve along an arc over the rising sun-dot ("everything your mind needs").
  - Characters replace letters inside words: a pair of eyes as the "oo" in "too," a smiling star as the "o" in "today."
  - Characters peek between words.
  - Negative self-talk ("I can't", "not good enough", "ughhhh") is set in rotated black Bold on torn strips, as a texture behind a photo.
  - Stacked words fade in tone ("stress / feelings / family / break ups / big goals").
- **Fallbacks:** Source Sans for web and email, Roboto, Arial in Google Slides/Docs. UI screens always use Headspace Aperçu.

## Device(s) / graphic language
1. **Faces library (the characters).** Faces are black line features drawn with round-capped strokes of one weight, placed on flat coloured shapes. The **hero face** is two small downward arcs (closed, content eyes) above one wide shallow smile arc. It sits high on the shape, and is used first in any primary communication. **Other expressions** are built from the same parts:
   - open eyes (white discs with black pupils), for alert or surprised
   - half-lidded white eyes over a flat line mouth, for unimpressed or defeated
   - ">" and "<" squeezed eyes, for stressed or overwhelmed
   - one open eye and one closed, glancing sideways, for curious or sceptical
   - dizzy spiral pupils, for overwhelmed
   - closed eyes with a small drip, for sleepy or sad
   - happy upturned arcs with a big smile, for joy

   The guidelines group moods, e.g. Happy & calm / playful, Distracted / stressed / angry / anxious, Sad & defeated. Rules: consistent stroke width (not too thin or thick), never stretch the mouth on x or y, use only faces from the library, never repeat the same face in one scene, match the face to the subject, and don't overuse faces for enterprise audiences.
2. **Core shape library (the bodies).** About 16 flat, rounded-corner primitives: pill, lightning bolt, four-point sparkle, rounded triangle, zigzag, triple-stacked lobes, eight-point star, speech bubble, double lobe, crescent moon, U-arch, ring, stair steps, quarter-circle wedge, wide capsule. Characters are these shapes (plus tall arches, half-discs and blobs) with a face added. In the "emotions" key visual, half-discs sit side by side around the full hero dot like phases of a moon, each in its own colour with its own face.
3. **Illustration build rules.** Flat fills and no outlines. Depth comes only from a darker 400 "shadow" tone cut as a crescent or flat plane, and from dramatic scale contrast. Scenes are layered: clouds drift, water shimmers, trees sway. Big emotions are drawn as elevated metaphors, e.g. a plate stacked with blocks for "too much," or a wave over a beach for anxiety. The dot never gets accessories or objects to hold, and scenes are never busy.
4. **Shapes as frames.** Photos are masked into brand shapes (rounded rectangles, arches, cloud and blob silhouettes, tilted cards). The frames tilt slightly and overlap in pairs.
5. **Sprites.** Small animated illustration elements (sparkles, flowers, rainbows, speech bubbles, the dot) layered over photography and live action. They bridge real footage to the illustrated world. Use them sparingly.
6. **UI chips and speech bubbles.** Cream pill cards with an avatar, a message ("You can.") and a program tag, used in ads as proof of real human support.

## Imagery
- **Illustration** is described in the guidelines as the heart of the brand. It leads consumer and emotional communication, and all of it must come from the library.
- **Photography** was newly expanded for coaching, therapy and B2B. Style is "slice of life":
  - candid in-between moments ("glimmers" of acceptance and connection), natural window light, warm, never over-produced
  - real everyday people and families, doing normal things
  - ordinary settings: meditating on a train, a lunch break, at school
  - B2B shoots skew to workplaces: factory, warehouse, office, work-from-home, small business
- **Clinicians and guides** are shown as colour photos cut out against a brand colour.
- **Hard rule:** always add an illustrative layer (a shape frame, sprite or face) when using photography. Never run a photo alone.
- **Never:** posed generic lifestyle stock, misery or negativity, literal depictions of problems, meditation clichés.
- A headshot guide lets care partners self-shoot: natural light, simple background, face the camera, natural smile.

## Voice
- **Pillars:** hopeful, visionary, approachable, reliable. Personality words include playful, vibrant, compassionate, optimistic, honest, dependable. The brand's own metaphor is a hug for the mind.
- **Rules:**
  - write at about a 6th-grade reading level, with small words and short sentences
  - hope and help, never toxic positivity
  - use exclamation points rarely
  - make no lofty claims, and back stats with the scientific-claims database
  - speak to the whole person
  - say "mind" rather than "brain"
  - never use conditions as adjectives ("so OCD")
  - use inclusive and gender-neutral terms ("friends," "folks")
  - use "us," not "they"
  - B2B copy is confident and human, never cold productivity talk
- **Word swaps:** guru becomes expert, teacher or guide. "Get rid of stress" becomes stress less. Zen, chi, chakra, nirvana, karma, transcend, higher self and spiritual are out. "Inner city" gets the real city's name.
- **Sample lines (from applications):** Be kind to your mind · how are you today? · Plate too full? · Big day today? · From inner critic to inner champion · Stress less. Focus more. Sleep soundly.

## Applications
Shown: app screens (Today, Explore/Meditate, Care team, coach chat), OOH billboards, bus-shelter posters, a full bus wrap, Times Square/Nasdaq digital screens, Instagram stories and paid social, email newsletters, partner lockups, presentation decks, whitepapers, video lower thirds and end cards, logo animations.

**What varies:**
- Background colour sets the tone: orange for core, yellow for Care, blue and green for coaching, indigo for Sleep.
- The share of illustration to photography changes, from all-character consumer ads to photo-led coaching and B2B.
- How loud the type gets changes: characters inside words, arcs and rotated strips in campaigns, quiet sentence-case layouts in product.
- Which face is shown depends on the message.

**What stays fixed:**
- the dot and lowercase wordmark, placed in a bottom corner
- Headspace Aperçu in sentence case
- flat colour with no outlines
- one line-weight for faces
- cream #F9F4F2 as the neutral ground
- the rule that photos always get an illustrative layer

**Motion:**
- Frame rates: 12 fps for playful and narrative, 24 fps for relaxing, ambient and instructional (breath loops), 60 fps only in-app (Lottie, UI).
- Custom velocity curves, never default easy-ease. No lazy drifting.
- Text rises with gentle but brisk fades: titles reveal word by word, long copy line by line, breathing exercises letter by letter.
- The hero dot moves smoothly to evoke meditation (a "breathing dot" asset).
- Characters get small blinks and glances.
- End cards come in iris, splash and dot-com versions.

## What makes it ownable
- The orange dot with the closed-eye smile. It is recognisable even as just the three strokes of the face, and it is built into the typeface as a glyph.
- A family of flat shapes with a consistent one-weight face kit. Any shape plus these eyes and mouth reads as Headspace.
- Orange #FF7300 on warm cream, and orange/yellow/blue as a trio.
- Characters that live inside the headline type.

## What a generic version would have been
- A teal-and-navy "health" palette with a leaf, brain or speech-bubble icon.
- A geometric sans wordmark.
- Stock photos of smiling therapists with laptops, or soft watercolour sunsets and lotus shapes for the calm side.
- A separate clinical sub-brand in blue, next to a playful meditation brand in orange.
- "Your journey to wellness" copy.

Headspace instead kept one mascot-led world and widened its emotional range, using the same characters for joy, stress and sleep.

## Principles it demonstrates
1. When the business widens, widen the emotional range of the existing mark instead of replacing it: give the icon siblings with other feelings.
2. Build characters from a closed kit (a shape library plus a face library with one stroke weight) so anyone can make new ones that still look native.
3. Make the logo's imperfection a stated value, but keep the illustrated version geometric so the two never blur.
4. Show hard feelings through metaphor and the essence of an emotion, never literal misery, so a sensitive category stays approachable.
5. Let photography carry trust and illustration carry feeling, and require an illustrative layer on every photo so the two stay one system.
6. Split the palette by offering (Core, Care, Sleep) with published proportions and an accessibility-tested pair list, so the system varies without chaos.
