# Graza

- **Name:** Graza
- **Business:** DTC-then-retail extra virgin olive oil: single-origin, unblended Picual from Jaén, Spain, sold in squeeze bottles ("Sizzle" for cooking, "Drizzle" for finishing; later "Frizzle" and others)
- **Place:** United States (founders Andrew Benin and Allen Dushi); oil from Jaén, Spain
- **Year:** Launched January 2022
- **Studio:** Gander, Brooklyn (Katie Levy, Mike McVicar): brand, packaging, illustration, website

## Brief & business truth

Olive oil in the US splits into precious bottles too expensive to use freely and cheap jugs of doubtful "extra virgin". Graza sells high-quality, fresh, single-origin oil at an in-between price in a squeezable restaurant-kitchen bottle. Gander's survey of hundreds of shoppers found: the top purchase cues are "extra virgin", a reasonable price and nice packaging; under 1% could say what extra virgin means; most had no brand preference. So there was room for the first universally loved olive oil brand, one that had to earn trust (education) while making the product feel usable, not precious.

## Strategy / insight

"Fancy oil in a decidedly un-fancy format": a union of highbrow and lowbrow. People want permission to stop saving the good oil. Position Graza as honest and fun, with quality proven through plain explanation rather than through faux-heritage signals.

## The idea

**One sentence:** Good olive oil should be used every day, in every way, so everything about the brand (the squeeze bottle, the product names, the drawings) says "use it" instead of "save it".
**Source:** benefit / use (the product named and designed by what you do with it), achieved by reversing olive-oil category codes.

## Register

Friendly pop / homemade charm with an old-world accent; bold, tonal, text-rich, cartoon-led.

## Category codes it used or broke

- Broke: faux heraldry, crests, European flags, gold foil, dark glass, Italian-nonna nostalgia, pastoral estate imagery as the main message.
- Broke: the precious single bottle. A plastic squeeze bottle from chef kitchens becomes the icon.
- Used (reframed): old-world letterforms to say "European" without flags; green as the category colour, but pushed to neon and lime saturation so it pops on shelf and screen.

## Mark

- **Type:** custom wordmark.
- **Construction:** five caps G-R-A-Z-A in a heavy, bespoke display face with flared, pinched stems (swelling at top and bottom, narrowing at the waist) and soft ball-and-teardrop terminals that read as drips of oil. Letters from R to the final A are fused along a continuous baseline slab, so the word sits like one poured shape. The Z's top-left and bottom-right terminals end in round droplets; A's counters are narrow slits; the G has a flat, spurred bar. Inspired by old-world (Spanish) letterforms.
- **Variants:** straight wordmark; wordmark set on the diagonal on the bottle; wordmark with ® at small sizes; the olive-with-drip roundel (an olive with a leaf and a drip "G" cut into it) used on labels and as an icon (observed).

## Wordmark / lettering

Custom, all caps, tight fit; never recoloured into multiple colours (one ink: dark olive, or dark on bright) (observed).

## Type system

- **Custom wordmark:** brand only.
- **ITC Garamond Condensed:** headlines and claims ("Squeezable Single-Origin Extra Virgin Olive Oil.", "Always fresh, never blended."), light, tightly set, sentence case with full stops.
- **GT Alpina Typewriter:** body, labels, recipe cards, small facts: a plain, honest, documentary voice.
- Circular text rings ("Always fresh · Single origin · Never blended") act as seals in place of heraldic badges (observed).
- Labels are deliberately text-heavy with little white space: expertise and enthusiasm, like a chef's condiment bottle.

## Colour system

No hexes published. Observed roles: dark olive green (bottle body, wordmark, drawings, the anchor); neon/bright green (Drizzle label, T-shirts, web panels); lime/acid yellow-green (Sizzle label, pattern grounds, tape); peachy sand (website and print ground). Pairs: dark olive on neon, dark olive on lime, dark olive on sand. Everything is tonal green plus sand; no red, gold or flag colours.

## Device(s) / graphic language

- **Character cast:** hand-drawn marker cartoons (drawn on sticky notes) of people, olives with drips, hands, pans, flames, a bottle doing a handstand, a person grinding olives; line-only in dark olive, occasional solid fills; animated as looping pictograms.
- **Pattern tape:** the cast repeated on a lime band wrapping boxes.
- **Round-cornered photo frames** and recipe-card layouts for web and social.
- **Graz-o-pedia:** an education series explaining what extra virgin means, finishing vs cooking oil, etc.

## Imagery

Casual, warm, flash-lit and handheld; shot with camcorders and disposable cameras for spontaneous moments: oil squeezed onto toast, pasta sauce, salad bowls, chicken cutlets; also olive trees in Jaén. Unexpected uses (oil on ice cream). Never: reverent dark still lifes of a bottle beside bread and a sprig of rosemary.

## Voice

Honest, funny, conversational, deflating snobbery. Product names by use: Sizzle, Drizzle, Frizzle. Lines like "this olive oil is special, but it shouldn't be", "9,000 years old. Never not fresh."; portmanteau names (the blog is the "Glog", the explainer the "Graz-o-pedia"). Explain jargon plainly; short sentences ending in full stops.

## Applications

Squeeze bottles (Sizzle/Drizzle), refill cans and boxes, shipping boxes with pattern tape, website and e-commerce, recipes, social, billboards (Brooklyn), T-shirts and merch, animated pictograms.
**What varies:** label colour per product, illustration scene, photo subject. **What stays fixed:** dark olive bottle, wordmark, Garamond Condensed + Alpina Typewriter, the cartoon line style, tonal greens + sand.

## Guidelines structure

None published.

## What makes it ownable

The squeeze bottle in dark olive with neon/lime labels; the drip-terminal wordmark; use-named products; the marker-drawn cast.

## What a generic version would have been

A tall dark glass bottle, gold foil crest, script "Olio" with an olive branch, a Spanish/Italian flag ribbon, terracotta and cream palette, estate photography, "cold-pressed since 1890".

## Principles it demonstrates

1. Name products after the use, so the name teaches how to buy and use them.
2. Signal origin through letterforms, not flags or crests.
3. Replace heritage badges with plain education when the audience cannot judge quality themselves.
4. Own the category colour by pushing it to an unexpected saturation rather than abandoning it.
5. Let the packaging format carry the brand's attitude; design the identity around the object people hold.
