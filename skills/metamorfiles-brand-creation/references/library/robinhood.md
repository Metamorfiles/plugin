# Robinhood

- **Name / business / place / year / studio:** Robinhood Markets (commission-free trading app grown into a full financial platform: stocks, options, crypto, retirement/IRA, cash sweep, credit card, Robinhood Gold subscription). Menlo Park, California, USA. Launched September/October 2024. Identity by PORTO ROCHA (New York; founders Felipe Rocha and Leo Porto) with Robinhood's in-house brand team (VP Marketing Michael Goodbody). ## Brief & business truth
Robinhood won its first decade as the scrappy, gamified app that made trading free for young retail investors, with a loud, illustrated, futuristic look. By 2024 it had become a multi-product wealth platform chasing older, richer, "serious" investors (IRA matching, Gold card, 3% cash), and it still carried reputational baggage from the 2021 meme-stock era. The fintech category had meanwhile converged on the very look Robinhood pioneered: bright rainbow palettes, chunky sans, playful illustration. The identity had to signal maturity and trust without losing the disruptive edge, and scale across many products and a premium tier.

## Strategy / insight
In a sea of fintech sameness, less is more: the way to stand out among colourful look-alikes is subtraction and precision. Move from "disruption" (futurism, noise) to "enduring trust" (restraint, craft), while keeping one electric signal of the challenger.

## The idea (one sentence)
Grow up by subtraction: strip Robinhood to black, warm greys and a single electric Robin Neon, with a feather that now visibly contains an upward arrow and photography that treats money as precise, surreal objects.
**Source:** reversal of category codes (fintech rainbow and cartoon illustration turned into near-monochrome luxury restraint), anchored in the name (the Robin Hood feather) and the benefit (growth: the arrow).

## Register
Strict minimal / material luxury: dark, quiet, precise, with gallery-grade still life and one neon accent.

## Category codes it used or broke
- Broke: fintech rainbow palettes, bubbly sans, cheerful people-with-phones illustration. Replaced by black, warm neutrals and an old-style serif, codes borrowed from private banking and luxury.
- Kept but sharpened: the neon challenger colour (a new yellow-green instead of the old Robinhood green) so it still reads as tech, not a bank.
- Used, reframed: the rising arrow and financial charts, but only as a hidden arrow in the feather and as technical line illustration, never as clip-art growth graphics.

## Mark
- **Type:** symbol (feather) + wordmark lockup; symbol used alone as app icon.
- **Construction:** a stylised feather leaning ~40–45° to the upper right. Its quill is a tapering curved stroke rising from a sharp point at lower left. The vane is a single solid shape with faceted straight edges (chamfered polygon) and a rounded tip at upper right. A thin negative-space line separates quill from vane and ends in an open chevron, so the gap itself draws an upward-right arrow. The earlier version's lower-right barb was removed, leaving a clean flat diagonal cut at the bottom of the vane (per 1000logos).
- **Optical details:** the arrow exists only in negative space at a hairline width, so it reads as a feather at a glance and as an arrow on second look; the vane's straight chamfers keep it technical rather than organic.
- **Variants:** horizontal lockup with the feather left of the wordmark (moved back to the left, its original position, per 1000logos); feather alone in Robin Neon on black (app icon, phone screens); embossed/metallic gold feather on a dark gold tile for Robinhood Gold; debossed on a metal card.

## Wordmark / lettering
"Robinhood" in upper/lower case, set in the custom Robinhood (RH) Phonic sans at a medium weight, normal-to-slightly-tight tracking. Letterforms were polished: rounder bowls and shoulders in b, n, h, d, with Phonic's small ink traps visible at large sizes. No ligatures or modified characters beyond the typeface's own details.

## Type system
- **Robinhood Phonic (RH Phonic):** the workhorse sans for UI, body, labels and lockups. Its signature is delicate ink traps at joins (the vertices of M, the crotches of n/h), which the guidelines highlight with neon dots. Precise but with character, replacing a characterless previous sans.
- **Martina Plantijn** (Klim Type Foundry, a contemporary take on Plantin): headline serif, warm and literate, used large and centred for financial nouns and numbers ("Market Index ETF ↑6.51% Gold NASDAQ 3% Cash"), and for premium Gold messaging ("Trade. Invest. Earn.").
- Rules visible in applications: serif for statements and product names, sans for everything functional; headlines short, sentence case; numerals and arrows set in the serif as display (inferred from layouts).

## Colour system (published)
- **Robin Neon** #CCFF00 (RGB 204/255/0, CMYK 25/0/100/0, PMS 380 C): accent and signal; the feather, key numbers, charts, one full-bleed poster or stage at a time.
- **Robin Black** #1C180D (RGB 28/24/13, PMS Black 2 C): a warm near-black, the dominant ground.
- **Heather #1** #8A8783 (PMS Warm Gray 8 C), **Heather #2** #B4B1AB (PMS Warm Gray 4 C), **Heather #3** #D4D0C9 (PMS Warm Gray 1 C): warm neutrals for grounds and type.
- White.
- Gold tones (metallic, not flat) for the Robinhood Gold sub-brand.
- Proportions (inferred): black and heathers dominate; Neon appears as a small spot (icon, a figure, a ring) on most pieces and as a full field only on hero posters and stage screens.

## Device(s) / graphic language
- **Technical illustration:** thin monoline, wireframe 3D objects in neon or black (stacked rings forming a funnel/tornado, stacked squares forming a stepped pyramid), inspired by financial charts and data; placed alone, centred, lots of space.
- **Modular layout:** content in clean blocks with wordmark small in a corner and URL opposite; big empty fields.
- **Neon moiré / concentric patterns** for large screens (investor conference stage).
- **Ink-trap dots:** small neon dots marking ink traps, used as a detail in type presentations.

## Imagery
Still-life photography by Lauren Bamford: one object, centred, on a dark or grey gradient seamless, hard studio light, crisp metallic surfaces, black-and-white or muted colour, each a visual metaphor for a money idea (a spring under a shoe for bounce/returns, a spinning bike cassette for momentum, watering cans big and small for growth, a compass/divider for precision, interlocked carabiners for security, a glass sphere). Also surreal colour moments (a mantis on a red balloon against flat blue sky). Never: smiling people with phones, stock traders, coins or rockets.

## Voice
Short, calm, declarative, confident: "Explore the world of investing." "Build your portfolio." "Build your financial future." "Trade. Invest. Earn." The newsroom summarises the goal as bold and cutting-edge but sophisticated and focused, distraction-free.

## Applications
App icon and phone screens, Apple Watch complication (Roth IRA balance with neon ring), city posters and digital six-sheets, billboards, lobby screens, tablet UI, investor conference stage, San Francisco campus glass signage, metal card with embossed feather, Robinhood Gold app icon and billboards.
What varies: ground (black / heather / neon), serif vs sans lead, object photo vs line illustration, gold vs neon for the sub-brand. What stays fixed: the feather, RH Phonic, the neon as the only saturated colour, one subject per frame, generous emptiness.

## Guidelines structure
No public guidelines; the case study shows guideline-style pages for colour (with hex/RGB/CMYK/PMS), typography (ink-trap detail), logo, illustration and photography (inferred as the internal sections).

## What makes it ownable
Robin Neon #CCFF00 on warm black; the feather with its negative-space arrow; single-object still-life photography; the pairing of a literary serif with an ink-trapped sans.

## What a generic version would have been
A rounded geometric sans, a teal-to-purple gradient, 3D coin and rocket illustrations, charts with green up-arrows, and lifestyle photos of young people smiling at phones: the look most fintechs adopted after Robinhood.

## Principles it demonstrates
- When your category copies you, differentiate by subtraction: fewer colours, fewer elements, more space.
- Keep one saturated signal colour and ration it, so a single spot of it identifies the brand.
- Put the benefit (growth) into the mark's negative space, where it rewards a second look instead of shouting.
- Pair a warm serif with a precise sans to move a tech brand up-market without losing its product clarity.
- Give photography a single rule (one object, one light, one metaphor) so every image is recognisably yours.
- Let a premium tier differ by material (metallic gold) rather than by a new logo.
