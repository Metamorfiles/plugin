# Museu Nacional da República

- **Name:** Museu Nacional da República (Museu Nacional Honestino Guimarães), known in the identity simply as MUSEU
- **Business:** public contemporary art museum inside Oscar Niemeyer's white concrete dome on the Esplanada dos Ministérios; free entry
- **Place:** Brasília, Brazil
- **Year:** 2020 (Brand New review 17 June 2020; It's Nice That July 2020)
- **Studio:** Porto Rocha (New York; Leo Porto, Felipe Rocha; strategy Elisa Mendes), invited by the Brasília agency Manufatura, which led the rebrand and wrote the "SEU MUSEU" line

## Brief & business truth
A landmark building (inaugurated 2006) that most people knew from the outside but did not enter. The museum wanted more visits, partners and revenue, and to shed the feeling that art museums are intimidating and elitist. The identity had to work along the whole visitor journey: city streets, social, the plaza, the dome interior, staff, shop.

## Strategy / insight
The building is already the brand everyone recognises, and the museum's problem is the threshold between the open plaza outside and the art inside. So the identity is about the relationship between inside and outside, and it speaks as "your museum" in plain, warm words while looking utilitarian and civic, like Brasília's own modernist signage.

## The idea (one sentence)
The aerial plan of Niemeyer's dome (a circle within a square plot) becomes a porthole that shows what is inside the museum to people outside it.
**Source:** place (the building's plan seen from above), plus a people-facing line from the name (MUSEU contains SEU, "your").

## Register
Civic modernist / utilitarian Swiss-Brazilian: black and white, one grotesque, big flush-left type, a single geometric figure.

## Category codes used or broken
- Used: the architecture as symbol (common for architect-designed museums), black and white, modernist sans.
- Broken: instead of drawing the dome's silhouette (the obvious move), it uses the plan view as an abstract window; it drops the long official name to one everyday word, MUSEU; and it replaces institutional tone with second-person possessives.

## Mark
- **Symbol:** a solid black square with a white circle punched out of its centre. Measured on the main poster: circle diameter ≈ 87% of the square side, concentric, so the black reads as four heavy corners joined by thin bands at the midpoints of each side (inferred from measurement). Reads as dome-from-above, a lens, a portal.
- **Portal behaviour:** the circle is a live window: it holds photography of the dome, artworks, exhibition images, staff portraits, video, or sky; on the street banner the circle is left empty so real sky shows through.
- **Lockup:** symbol stacked above MUSEU set to the exact width of the square, then NACIONAL / DA REPÚBLICA small beneath, flush left. Small-size lockup on stationery: symbol at left, three-line name to its right.

## Wordmark / lettering
Founders Grotesk (Klim Type Foundry), regular weight, all caps. MUSEU is the primary word, set big; NACIONAL DA REPÚBLICA always subordinate at about a quarter of the size. No custom letter modifications; its round C, O, S echo the curved walls of the dome.

## Type system
- Founders Grotesk (display, caps, tight tracking, set so the word fills a column or the symbol width) and Founders Grotesk Text (information, sentence case, small, three equal columns: access / hours / social).
- Rule: flush left, ragged right, no centred type. Headlines are single words stacked (SEU / MUSEU / SEU / MUSEU; MUSEU / ABERTO; GUIA / 2020), each line a word.
- Type-as-image: word stacks and repetition (anagram SEU MUSEU) carry posters without pictures.

## Colour system
- Black and white core (published colours not given; no accent colour in core assets).
- Colour enters only through content inside the portal or the artwork (e.g. the 2020 guide cover uses an artwork's orange/green/blue; exhibition posters take a photograph's wood yellow as full background).
- Sky blue appears as found colour (real sky through the empty circle, or blue sky photography), not as a brand swatch.

## Device(s) / graphic language
- **Portal circle:** crops any image to a circle inside the black square; the image changes, the square frame never does. Can be used huge (poster, 80% of height) or tiny (stationery).
- **Stacked-word headline:** one word per line, left-aligned, same size, often repeated.
- **Three-column footer:** fixed information strip at the bottom of every poster: Entrada gratuita + address / Funcionamento hours / Siga o MUSEU + #seuMUSEU.
- **Exhibition sub-brand:** exhibition title set in the same caps top-left, symbol top-right with MUSEU below it, full-bleed or boxed photo below.

## Imagery
Architecture photography of the dome and its white ramps (bright, high-key, clean blue sky), artwork reproductions, and portraits of staff and visitors in the white interior. Photography shows real people using the space (reception desk, mediator badge, visitor leaning on a ramp). Never: dark moody gallery shots or decorative illustration.

## Voice
Warm, direct, second person, in Portuguese. "SEU MUSEU" (your museum) used as the brand sign-off and hashtag (#seuMUSEU); short announcements like "MUSEU ABERTO"; "Siga o MUSEU" (follow the museum). The institution refers to itself as "o MUSEU", not by its full title. At most two or three words per headline.

## Applications
Lamp-post banners, bus-shelter posters, wall poster triptychs, exhibition posters (33ª Bienal de São Paulo itinerância), Instagram posts, visitor guide, staff ID badges (portrait in the circle), business cards, letterhead, envelopes, wayfinding and exhibition signage inside the dome, uniforms, merchandise, packaging, website.
**What varies:** the content in the portal, the stacked words, photo vs no photo. **What stays fixed:** black square with circle, Founders Grotesk caps, flush-left, the bottom information strip, black and white.

## Guidelines structure
Not published. The case study shows the system components: symbol, lockups, portal, type hierarchy, templates for posters and social, stationery, environmental.

## What makes it ownable
The black square with the circular hole (instantly the dome from above), and the repeated SEU MUSEU word stack. Either alone identifies the museum in Brasília.

## What a generic version would have been
A thin-line drawing of the dome's silhouette with the full name in a light geometric sans, a colourful "vibrant culture" palette, and institutional copy ("Museu Nacional da República apresenta...").

## Principles it demonstrates
- Draw the building from a view nobody uses (plan, not elevation), so the symbol becomes abstract enough to hold other things.
- Make the symbol a container: a fixed frame with changing content gives both recognition and endless variation.
- Shorten the name to the word people actually say, and let the rest sit small.
- Keep the brand palette black and white and let the content supply colour.
- Find the voice inside the name (MUSEU / SEU) so the tagline feels inevitable rather than added.
