## Brief & business truth
The founder is a dentist who kept recommending toothpastes full of artificial ingredients and abrasive whiteners, almost all in plastic (the studio cites over 300 million tubes landfilled a year in the UK). She wanted an effective, natural, plastic-free toothpaste that could break a category of blue, white and red. The studio framed the problem as: how do you make people care about sustainable toothpaste, and how do you look trustworthy and effective while carrying energy the category has never had.

## Strategy / insight
Brushing is a twice-daily ritual, a fixed bookend to chaotic days. The identity pairs clinical clarity (trust) with spirit (character). Messaging is clear, short and transparent; the design strips everything back so the sensibility is the difference, not the claims.

## The idea (one sentence) and its source
Toothpaste presented as a calm, honest, type-only object in one soft colour per job, with real imperfect smiles in place of dazzling white teeth. **Source:** reversal of category codes (every oral-care convention inverted), grounded in the founder's dentist credibility.

## Register
Strict minimal, type-led, pastel clinical.

## Category codes it used or broke
- Broke: red/blue/white palettes, swooshes and sparkles, "9 out of 10 dentists" claim badges, 3D-rendered paste stripes, plastic tubes in printed cartons, perfect-white-teeth models.
- Kept (quietly): a colour per variant (a shelf code people understand), the words "Fluoride Toothpaste" in plain view, a white variant for Whitening, clinical cleanliness through white space and rules.

## Mark
- Wordmark only: PAIST in uppercase LL Supreme (Lineto; a humanist reworking of Futura). Geometric construction: pointed apex A with a low crossbar, straight-sided I, S with round terminals, T with a wide bar. Normal tracking, no modifications visible (inferred as unmodified type).
- Used at extreme scale running vertically up the side of the box (the wordmark crosses the full height of the spine), and small, horizontal at the top of every label.
- Embossed on the metal squeezer key's round head and debossed around the tube cap: the five letters P-A-I-S-T spaced evenly around the circular cap face, so the cap itself is a small signature.

## Wordmark / lettering
Uppercase for the name only; everything else in sentence case in the same family.

## Type system
- One family, LL Supreme, for everything: wordmark, variant names, product descriptors, social posts, website.
- Hierarchy by size alone: medium weight throughout, large for headlines ("Myth Busting: Fluoride"), small for specs ("100% Plastic Free Tube", "Mild Mint", "Spearmint").
- Specification as display: a poster sets "100% Plastic-Free / 75ml / 2.64 fl oz / Fluoride (1450ppm)" centred and big, turning regulatory text into the headline.
- Dictionary-entry format for education posts: term, word class (NOUN), numbered definitions.

## Colour system
One solid colour per tube, per variant; no gradients, no second colour on a tube.
- Whitening: white.
- Daily: turquoise blue, about #69C4D9 (sampled from published graphics, inferred).
- Sensitive: lavender, about #9F8EC4 (sampled, inferred).
- Accessory (squeezer key box): warm mid grey.
- Type and rules: near-black. Backgrounds: warm off-white, about #F9F8F6 (sampled), or the variant colour itself.
- Silver/bare aluminium of the tube shoulder, cap and key is treated as a brand colour (inferred).

## Device(s) / graphic language
1. **The banded label:** each tube and box is divided by thin horizontal black rules into three stacked bands: name (PAIST) at the top; variant (Whitening/Daily/Sensitive) in the middle band; descriptor ("Fluoride Toothpaste" plus flavour) at the bottom. Text is centred in each band. The same band structure is reused for social tiles (rule above and below a text block).
2. **Inverted end:** on the tube the bottom band is set upside down relative to the top, so the tube reads correctly whether it stands on its cap or lies crimp-up (visible in the squeeze shots).
3. **The crumpled tube:** product shots celebrate the aluminium crumpling as it's used; the label's rules bend with the metal.
4. **Metal objects:** cap and squeezer key carry the letters in relief.

## Imagery
- Product: soft-lit still life on pale grey sweeps and ledges, one or three tubes, diagonal flat lays, extreme close-ups of cap and key in chrome.
- People: tight, frontal close-ups of real faces laughing, grimacing, grinning; visible gaps between teeth, braces, freckles, uneven teeth. Backgrounds in the palette's blue or neutral. Never whitened, never perfect.
- Never: paste-stripe renders, sparkle graphics, lab coats.

## Voice
Plain, transparent and a little mischievous: "Myth Busting: Fluoride", dictionary definitions of ingredients (potassium citrate), specs stated as facts. Short noun phrases on pack. No superlatives or dentist-approval percentages. BP&O notes DTC language that avoids leaning into generic millennial minimalism.

## Applications
Tubes (three variants), cartons, squeezer key and its box, embossed cap, social tiles (education and specs), portrait campaign, motion for social and web, website (desktop and mobile).
- **Fixed:** one typeface, the banded layout with rules, one colour per variant, centred text.
- **Varies:** scale and orientation of the wordmark (small top band vs. vertical full-height on the box spine), the background colour, the product's physical state (new vs. crumpled).

## Guidelines structure
None published.

## What makes it ownable
The ruled three-band label; one soft flat colour per tube in aluminium; PAIST running vertically up the box; letters around the cap; unretouched smiles.

## What a generic version would have been
A "natural" toothpaste with a leaf or tooth icon, a green-and-white palette or the category's red-blue-white, a script or rounded wordmark, claim bursts ("SLS free!", "Dentist approved"), and glossy models with bleached teeth.

## Principles it demonstrates
- List the category's codes and invert them one by one, but keep the single code that aids shopping (colour per variant).
- Let one typeface and one rule structure do the whole system; hierarchy by size, not by more fonts.
- Turn required information (specs, ingredients) into the headline content.
- Show the product in real use (crumpled, squeezed) when the material is the story.
- Cast imperfection (real teeth) as proof of honesty in a category built on retouched perfection.
