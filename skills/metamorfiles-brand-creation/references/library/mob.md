# Mob (formerly Mob Kitchen)

- **Name / business / place / year / studio:** Mob, a food media brand founded in 2014 by Ben Lebus as Mob Kitchen (cheap, easy recipes for students and young people, first known for "feed four for under £10" videos on Instagram and YouTube), now a platform with recipe app/website, series with guest chefs, cookbooks, newsletters, events and a food truck. London, UK. Rebrand launched September 2021 after ten months of work (dropping "Kitchen"). Studio NARI (London; creative director Caterina Bianchini). Motion with Connor Campbell Studio. Typeface: Right Grotesk (Pangram Pangram), customised.

## Brief & business truth
Mob grew from a student recipe account into one of the UK's fastest-growing food media platforms, with many series, chefs, formats and products. The original identity no longer fitted and the brand had drifted from its mission: teaching young people food they will actually cook. Asked for a refresh, NARI first reworked mission, vision, values and voice with the founder, then rebuilt the whole identity. The hard part, per NARI, was scale: dozens of series and personalities that each needed their own feel under one brand.

## Strategy / insight
Mob is less a kitchen than a crowd: the community already called itself "the Mob". The essence ("Mobness") is the lively, messy dinner party among friends, a table of different characters (the one with sauce on their shirt, the one who adds ketchup to everything, the one who gets political) who together are one Mob. Cooking is not about perfection.

## The idea (one sentence)
One Mob round one table: a stark black-and-white master brand is the communal table, and every series, chef and product is a different guest at it, free to be loud in its own way, while the logo itself is a splodge of sauce.
**Source:** people (the community name "the Mob", sharpened by dropping "Kitchen") and the moment (a messy dinner with friends).

## Register
Vernacular / loud display: youthful, maximal in the sub-system, with a disciplined minimal master brand.

## Category codes it used or broke
- Broke: food-media codes of rustic kraft textures, handwritten script, chalkboard, gingham or the glossy "chef authority" look. Mob uses a Swiss-ish grotesk, black and white, and pop-culture stickers.
- Broke: perfection. The letterforms have deliberately inconsistent rounding and the mark is a splat.
- Used, on purpose: tabloid/pop merch codes (tattoo hearts with banners, tribal flourishes, flames, 3D extruded stickers) to talk like its audience.
- Used: appetite-driven food photography, but framed by clean type and lots of flat colour.

## Mark
- **Type:** symbol (sauce squiggle / m) + lowercase wordmark "mob".
- **Symbol construction:** a single continuous white blob that reads as a wobbly lowercase m, like sauce dragged across a counter. Left end: a fat rounded loop; the stroke swells and pinches, dropping to hairline-thin sharp points at the bottoms of the valleys (calligraphic contrast, as if a spoon lifted off). In the centre sits one straight, slightly tilted parallelogram stroke with flat-cut ends, the only rigid element, like a knife stroke through the sauce. The right end closes in another rounded loop. Overall proportion about 3:2 wide, sits slightly rotated.
- **Optical details:** contrast between swollen curves and needle-thin joins gives motion; the one straight stroke stops it looking like a generic squiggle.
- **Variants:** flat white on black, reversed; turned into glossy 3D inflated stickers (yellow, orange) with highlights; used as a pattern on merch and the truck.

## Wordmark / lettering
"mob" in lowercase, very wide, extra-heavy grotesk (from Right Grotesk's wide/black end), drawn to look "full", like letters stuffed with food: tiny counters, squat x-height, tight spacing. Rounding of corners is intentionally inconsistent. Used flat in black or white, or in brand colours on grounds of other brand colours; endlessly restyled on stickers (3D extruded, on a tattoo banner, in flames, in a tribal-tattoo frame).

## Type system
- **Right Grotesk** (Pangram Pangram) customised; NARI used three of its variable axes (width, weight, slant/italic) so a single line can shift between compressed, wide, italic and black.
- Signature move: recipe titles set one word per line, each line at a different width/weight/style (e.g. regular, regular, italic, black wide), so a dish name reads like a table of different characters.
- Upper and lower case, sentence case; tight leading; titles top-left on warm off-white or flat colour.
- Small text in the same family for UI chips (New Recipes, Trending, Ingredients, Nutrition) as black pills.

## Device(s) / graphic language
- **Recipe badges:** circular black-outline or solid-black roundels: chef name in an ellipse (like a plate), diet (VG, GF, DF), time as a partial ring with minutes (a clock/progress arc). Clustered they become a pattern.
- **Series micro-brands:** each series or show gets its own logotype with its own personality depending on the chef or format: "meets" (outlined letters in a pill on orange), "Giveaway" (warped serif on violet), "Guest" (distorted chunky lettering on acid), "make it with" (hand-warped script on raspberry).
- **Stickers and flavour words:** flat colour pills and circles with adjectives (Spicy, Garlicky, Zingy, Gooey, Tasty, Squadge, Punchy, X Hot); stickers of the wordmark in pop styles.
- **Cut-out ingredients:** single ingredients isolated on white or colour, arranged in a grid (Mob 6 billboard: six ingredients).

## Imagery
Close, overhead or 45° food photography, abundant, messy and saucy (drips, pours, crumbs), shared tables with many hands; natural daylight or moody contrast; also cut-out ingredients. People shown as a crowd at events or cooks' hands in action. Never pristine plated fine dining (inferred).

## Voice
Playful, bold, descriptive, cheeky; lots of appetite adjectives. "Food they'll actually cook." "Tasty 6-Ingredient Meals." Series and merch lines are short and punchy ("Spicy" on a red tote).

## Applications
App and website (recipe cards, category chips, chef avatars), Instagram, cookbooks (Mob 6), monthly/weekly newsletters and recipe books, billboards, sticker sheets, tote bags and aprons, office and test kitchen, events and street-food stall, the Mob truck.
What varies: series logotypes, colour fields, sticker styles, photography mood. What stays fixed: black-and-white master brand (mark + "mob"), Right Grotesk, the badge system, the named flavour palette.

## Guidelines structure
Not public. The case shows: strategy (mission, values, voice), master brand (mark, wordmark), typography, colour palette with names and hex, badges/iconography, series sub-brand system, photography/art direction, motion, applications.

## What makes it ownable
The sauce-splat m; the squat, stuffed lowercase "mob"; recipe titles in mixed widths; the badge roundels; flavour-named colours.

## What a generic version would have been
A handwritten script logo with a whisk or spoon, kraft paper and chalkboard textures, a warm terracotta-and-olive palette, and every series using the same template with a different photo.

## Principles it demonstrates
- Separate a calm master brand from a loud, permissive secondary system so many voices can live under one name.
- Build imperfection into the letterforms when the brand's truth is "not about perfection".
- Name colours after what the brand is about (flavours) so the palette carries the subject.
- Turn functional metadata (chef, diet, time) into a graphic device people learn to read.
- Let one typeface family change width, weight and slant within a single line to express variety without adding faces.
- Shorten the name when the audience already calls you something shorter.
