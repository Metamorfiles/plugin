# Castil

- **Name:** Castil (a brand of Gimetal Srl)
- **Business:** family-scale Italian manufacturer of bent-steel-tube and wire furniture (chairs, stools, tables, mostly contract and outdoor), made in its own factory, with designs by outside studios (e.g. AtelierNanni, Studioventotto); powder-coated in a range of colours; buyers are architects, interior designers, hospitality
- **Place:** Aiello del Friuli (Udine), Italy; studio in Palma de Mallorca and London
- **Year:** 2025 (studio case study April 2025; It's Nice That profile 18 June 2025; also featured by Creative Review and Communication Arts; Adobe MAX Creativity Awards 2025 honoree per the studio)
- **Studio:** HONDO (María Vioque, Fran Méndez), typeface Castil Mono by Ignacio Casco with Best Typefaces

## Brief & business truth
Castil makes its products in-house by bending steel tube and rod, then powder-coats them in bright, customisable colours. The old logo was decorative and split the name in two, and said nothing about how the furniture is made. The new identity had to speak to architects and specifiers (technical, precise) while showing the playful colour range, and work across catalogues, product sheets, packaging, labels, website and motion.

## Strategy / insight
The manufacturing process is the brand: a steel tube bent into a continuous line with a constant diameter and rounded bends. If the letters are drawn the way the chairs are made, every word Castil sets becomes a small product demonstration. Monospacing brings in the order and modularity of industrial production and the look of technical data sheets.

## The idea (one sentence)
A custom monospaced typeface whose letters are drawn as bent steel tube, so the name and every spec sheet look fabricated in the Castil factory.
**Source:** product-craft (the steel-bending process).

## Register
Industrial-playful / technical data aesthetic: monospaced type, spec-sheet labels, kraft and steel, plus bright product colours.

## Category codes used or broken
- Used: product-led photography, catalogue with specs, neutral base.
- Broken: most Italian furniture brands use a refined high-contrast serif or a thin luxury sans with lifestyle photography; Castil uses a friendly, rounded monospace and presents chairs like parts with a tag, with factory data (tube diameter, weight, colour code) as a design feature.

## Mark
- **Type:** lowercase wordmark "castil" set in Castil Mono.
- **Construction (measured from the master file):** monoline strokes of constant width (about 20% of x-height); all terminals fully rounded like tube ends; bends drawn as arcs of constant radius joining straight runs, as in a bent tube (the c is a rounded rectangle open on the right; the s is three straight horizontals joined by semicircular bends). The a is distinctive: a straight diagonal stroke leaning right replaces the usual vertical stem, meeting a flat-topped bowl, like a tube bent at an angle. The t has a short rounded crossbar and a tail bending into a horizontal foot; the l ends in a long horizontal foot bent at 90° with a rounded corner; the i has a round dot. Equal character widths (monospaced), so the narrow i and l get long feet and wide spacing.
- **Variants:** wordmark alone (white on near-black, black on white, grey tone on kraft); large cropped "ca" fragment used as a supergraphic on packaging; 3D chrome-tube rendering of the letters in motion.

## Wordmark / lettering
Castil Mono (proprietary, Ignacio Casco / Best Typefaces), lowercase for the logo; no separate logotype drawing beyond the font. Slanted a is the signature glyph.

## Type system
- Castil Mono does everything: logo, headlines, labels and data. Small caps-like uppercase labels in a lighter grey ("PRODUCT", "DESIGNED BY", "TUBE THICKNESS", "WEIGHT", "COLOUR", "DESCRIPTION") above values in black (label/value pairs).
- Tagline and descriptors in uppercase Mono, often rotated along edges: "METAL FURNITURE MAKERS", "MANUFACTURED FROM STEEL", "NEW PRODUCT RELEASE", "IN CASE OF LOSS, CONTACT".
- Rule (inferred): data is set as spec sheets with fixed two-column label/value grids.

## Colour system
- Neutral base: near-black (#231F20 approx. from the master logo file (inferred)), white, textured off-white paper, kraft brown, brushed/galvanised steel grey.
- Product colours enter as functional colour bands on the tags, each named with an Italian nickname plus the RAL number of the powder coat: Fragola 3018 (strawberry red), Sole 1018 (zinc yellow), Prato 6018 (yellow-green). The brand colours are literally the factory's coating chart.
- Proportion: mostly neutral; one saturated band or one coloured product per composition (inferred).

## Device(s) / graphic language
- **Notched label module:** every information block is a white rounded rectangle with small concave notches cut at the corners and bracket marks, stacked vertically into a tag (like die-cut tickets or pressed metal plates joined in a strip). Modules hold: logo, product data, colour band, description, product photo, company contact. The same module forms business cards, shipping labels, box labels and loose label strips.
- **Tube line:** flowing lines and circles derived from the chairs' silhouettes and tube bends (per Logos-World), and the chrome 3D tube lettering in films.
- **Supergraphic crop:** oversized partial letters ("ca") printed white on kraft boxes; grey packing tape printed with the wordmark and contact data.

## Imagery
Product photography of steel frames: close, cropped details of bent wire and tube; chairs against brushed stainless steel or white seamless backgrounds; coloured frames on white for catalogue cut-outs. Motion blur and steel reflections in films. Never: lifestyle terraces with people, warm Mediterranean clichés.

## Voice
Factual, industrial, bilingual English/Italian with a light wink: short descriptions ("Metal chair with armrests"), specs in units (ø16-8mm, 6 Kg), shipping language ("In case of loss, contact"), claims like "Metal Furniture Makers", "Manufactured from steel".

## Applications
Wordmark, product tags and tech cards, business cards, letterhead/shipping labels, kraft cartons and die-lines, printed packing tape, label strips, catalogue/editorial, website, social and motion films (chrome letters).
**What varies:** product, colour band (RAL), photo, the text in each module. **What stays fixed:** Castil Mono, lowercase wordmark, the notched module shape, neutral base materials.

## Guidelines structure
Not published. The case study presents: logo, typeface, module/label system, packaging, stationery, motion, website.

## What makes it ownable
The bent-tube letterforms (especially the slanted a and the footed l) and the notched stacked tags with RAL-named colour bands. Either would identify Castil in a trade-fair hall.

## What a generic version would have been
A thin, widely tracked uppercase CASTIL in a Didone or geometric sans, beige and terracotta palette, sunset lifestyle photography of outdoor chairs by a pool, "Made in Italy" roundel.

## Principles it demonstrates
- Draw the letters with the product's own manufacturing logic (constant diameter, rounded bends), so the type is a sample of the craft.
- Take colour names from the production process (RAL coating codes) and give them friendly names, so the palette is real, not invented.
- Make technical data a design material: label/value spec sheets can carry a brand's personality for a B2B audience.
- Build one modular container (the notched tag) and reuse it at every scale, from business card to shipping label.
- Keep the base neutral and industrial (kraft, steel, off-white) so the products supply the colour.
