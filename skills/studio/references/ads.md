# Ads

`design.md`, `images.md` and the `copywriting` skill still apply; this is what paid placements add.

## The set

An ad runs where the platform puts it, so make each placement the user buys, from one idea: the feed post (`instagram-post`, `facebook-post`, `linkedin-post`: 4:5) and the story or reel (`instagram-story`: 9:16). The idea is one, the composition is made for each shape (the `repurpose` skill for the second and later sizes). Ask which platforms only when the user hasn't said and the brief doesn't tell.

## What makes it work

- **One message.** An ad is seen for under two seconds in a scroll: one claim, one picture, one action.
- **The brand in the first look.** The logo or the small mark is visible without reading; the brand's colours and imagery do the rest. A viewer who doesn't stop should still know whose ad it was.
- **The offer or the action stated:** "Book a table", "20% off this week", "New: the night range". Never a vague "Learn more" when the action is concrete.
- **Little text on the image.** The headline and the action; everything else goes in the ad's caption (the variant's `caption`).
- **Readable at thumb size:** look at each frame at a quarter of its size before you hand it over.

## The platforms' covered areas

On 9:16 placements Meta covers part of the top, the bottom and the sides with its own interface: nothing that matters there. The presets carry these zones and the checks name them.

## Claims

No prices, discounts, dates, awards or results the user didn't give. An ad's claim is the user's to make: when one is missing, write the ad around what is true and ask for the rest.
