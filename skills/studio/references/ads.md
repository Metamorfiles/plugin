# Ads

`design.md`, `images.md` and the `copywriting` skill still apply; this is what paid placements add.

## The set

An ad runs where the platform puts it, so make each placement the user buys, from one idea: the feed post (`instagram-post`, `facebook-post`, `linkedin-post`: 4:5) and the story or reel (`instagram-story`: 9:16). The idea is one, the composition is made for each shape: one page whose design declares every placement's format. An ad that exists, into more sizes: the `repurpose` skill. Ask which platforms only when the user hasn't said and the brief doesn't tell.

## What makes it work

- **One message.** An ad is seen for a moment in a scroll: one claim, one picture, one action.
- **The brand in the first look.** The logo, or its mark, is visible without reading; the brand's colours and imagery do the rest. A viewer who doesn't stop should still know whose ad it was.
- **The offer or the action, stated as concretely as it is:** "Shop the serum", "New: the night range".
- **Little text on the image.** The headline and the action; everything else goes in the ad's caption (the variant's `caption`).
- **Built for thumb size:** the claim, the action and everything else that matters large enough to read on a phone at a glance.

## The platforms' covered areas

On 9:16 placements Meta covers part of the top, the bottom and the sides with its own interface: keep what matters clear of it. The presets carry these zones and the checks name them.

## Claims

No prices, discounts, dates, awards or results the user didn't give. An ad's claim is the user's to make: when one is missing, write the ad around what is true ("Grounding" in `references/copy.md` of the `copywriting` skill) and ask for the rest.
