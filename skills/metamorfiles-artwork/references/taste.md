# Artwork taste

What strong print and packaging artwork looks like. It describes; it never decides: the brief, the
direction and the category decide what the piece is.

## Strong

- **A picture that owns the face:** an illustration, a pattern or a scene drawn for this object,
  wrapping it, not a small drawing floating in a field of colour.
- **The brand's own hand:** the same line, fills, palette and character as the rest of the imagery,
  so the pack and the posters are plainly one brand.
- **One hero, read from a distance:** the brand or the product name leads, everything else is
  clearly smaller.
- **Type that belongs to the picture:** set where the picture left room, in the brand's faces, at
  sizes a shopper reads.
- **A system across a range:** flavours or variants as the same layout with a changed colour and
  picture, so the shelf reads as a family.

## Weak, and why it happens

- **Flat boxes of colour with type on them,** where the category carries pictures: the artwork was
  built in markup alone. Paint the picture.
- **The logo alone on a field of colour** on an object printed all over in real life.
- **Words drawn by the image model:** letters warp and change. Every word is set in step 3.
- **A picture with no room for the type,** and boxes laid under the words to save it: paint the
  picture again with calm zones.
- **A different style from the brand's imagery:** a glossy 3D scene on a brand drawn in flat
  sticker shapes, in the brand's image contract (`brand/imagery-guide.md`).
